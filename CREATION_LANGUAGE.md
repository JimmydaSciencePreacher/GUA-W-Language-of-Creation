# Creation Language
## Working Vocabulary and Distinctions

This document records the shared descriptive vocabulary being investigated in this repository. Definitions are working descriptions, not claims that every scientific phenomenon must contain every category. Each term must earn its usefulness through accurate application to evidence.

## Foundational terms

### Structure
**Working question:** What has been brought into an organized relationship?

Use *structure* to describe an arrangement of components, elements, or relations. In a scientific account, identify the actual components and the organization being described. Examples of scientific structures include molecular arrangements, crystal lattices, anatomical organization, and large-scale cosmic structures. These examples do not imply a common mechanism.

### Framework
**Working question:** What establishes, supports, or delimits the arrangement within which a structure exists or operates?

A framework may be physical, geometric, relational, energetic, or conceptual. State which kind is meant. Do not use *framework* as a vague substitute for cause, mechanism, environment, or theory.

### Pattern
**Working question:** What recurring arrangement, sequence, distribution, or relationship can be witnessed?

A pattern may be spatial, temporal, statistical, behavioral, or mathematical. Describe how recurrence was identified and whether it is robust, approximate, scale-dependent, or limited to a particular dataset. A pattern alone does not establish its cause.

### Constraint
**Working question:** What boundaries, conditions, limits, or governing relations shape the possible expression of a phenomenon?

Name the specific constraint: boundary condition, conservation law, resource limitation, geometry, temperature range, pressure, chemical availability, or another evidenced condition. Distinguish a constraint that limits possibilities from a mechanism that produces a result.

### Purpose
**Working question:** What function, role, or outcome is evident?

Use care. In biology, *function* may refer to an effect that contributes to the operation or persistence of a system, or to an evolutionary account of a trait. In engineering, purpose may be assigned by a designer. In other scientific contexts, a function may be mathematically defined. Do not infer conscious intention or ultimate purpose merely from usefulness, order, persistence, or outcome. Keep scientific functional claims distinct from theological claims.

### Manifestation
**Working question:** What becomes observable, measurable, or realized under stated conditions?

Identify the phenomenon and the conditions under which it appears. Specify whether manifestation means direct observation, instrument detection, a measured effect, an inferred state, or a model output. These are not interchangeable.

### Witness
**Working question:** What registers, detects, records, or reveals the result?

Identify the witness in the relevant scientific sense: observer, detector, instrument, sensor, sample, trace, image, spectrum, fossil, or reproducible record. State what it can register and what it cannot. The record is evidence about a phenomenon, not automatically the phenomenon in its entirety.

## Terms that must remain distinct

- **Component:** An identifiable part or entity within a system.
- **Relationship:** A specified connection or dependence between entities or variables.
- **Process:** A sequence of changes or interactions over time.
- **Mechanism:** A supported account of how a process occurs.
- **Condition:** A circumstance under which a phenomenon occurs or is measured.
- **Boundary:** An interface or limit between regions, states, or systems. It may be passive, active, or both; investigate rather than assume.
- **Environment:** Surrounding conditions and entities that may affect a system.
- **State:** A specified condition or configuration of a system at a given time.
- **Transition:** A change from one state or configuration to another.
- **Scale:** The spatial, temporal, or organizational level at which a phenomenon is described.
- **Evidence:** Observations, measurements, records, or results relevant to evaluating a claim.

## Rules for using the vocabulary

1. Retain the established scientific term beside the GUA-W description.
2. Define the particular meaning of a term when entering a new discipline.
3. Do not assume that the same word identifies the same mechanism in different fields.
4. Do not infer purpose from pattern, order, complexity, or outcome alone.
5. Do not infer causation from correlation or recurrence.
6. Do not treat an analogy as evidence of shared mechanism.
7. Record counterexamples and cases where a term does not fit.
8. Permit the vocabulary to develop in precision through investigation; do not force observations to fit an imprecise definition.

## Relationship comparison

When two phenomena appear related, classify the proposed connection:

- **Same mechanism:** Evidence supports the same causal process in both cases.
- **Comparable relationship:** Different mechanisms exhibit a clearly specified relational similarity.
- **Analogy:** A limited comparison helps describe an aspect but does not establish shared operation.
- **Surface resemblance:** Similar appearance or wording without demonstrated underlying correspondence.
- **Unresolved:** Available evidence does not yet justify a classification.

## Open vocabulary questions

- Does *framework* consistently identify something distinct from structure, environment, or boundary conditions?
- Under what circumstances is a boundary merely a separator, and when does it participate in transfer, regulation, or interaction?
- How should *purpose* be used across fields without confusing demonstrated function with inferred intention?
- When does a recurring pattern warrant a claim of underlying organization, and what evidence is needed?
- How should *witness* distinguish the phenomenon, the detector, the measurement, and the interpretation of the record?

These questions remain open until investigated.
