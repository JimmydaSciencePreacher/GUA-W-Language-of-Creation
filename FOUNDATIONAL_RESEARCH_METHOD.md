# Foundational Research Method
## GUA-W Language of Creation

### 1. Purpose

This repository begins a fresh investigation of the sciences. Its purpose is to examine scientific observations and ask whether relationships observed in nature can be described consistently through the foundational vocabulary first recognized in Genesis 1–2.

The project is not an attempt to replace scientific disciplines, rewrite their findings, or make a GUA-W description stand in for a scientific explanation. Scientific terminology, measurements, methods, mechanisms, uncertainty, and competing interpretations must remain visible.

The central investigative possibility is:

> Instead of continually translating Scripture into the language of science, perhaps we can learn to describe scientific discoveries in the language of Creation first recognized through Scripture.

This is a research possibility to examine, not a conclusion that every scientific phenomenon must fit every term.

### 2. Foundational vocabulary

The following are working descriptions. Their usefulness and limits must be tested against actual observations.

- **Structure:** What has been brought into an organized relationship?
- **Framework:** What establishes or supports the arrangement within which a structure exists or operates?
- **Pattern:** What recurring arrangement, sequence, distribution, or relationship can be witnessed?
- **Constraint:** What boundaries, conditions, limits, or governing relations shape possible expression?
- **Purpose:** What function, role, or outcome is evident? Distinguish an experimentally demonstrated function from an inferred purpose or theological claim.
- **Manifestation:** What becomes observable, measurable, or realized under the relevant conditions?
- **Witness:** What registers, detects, records, or reveals the result? Identify the actual observer, instrument, trace, or evidentiary record.

These terms are related but not interchangeable. An investigation must not assume that every structure has an independently demonstrable purpose, that every recurring pattern has the same cause, or that every boundary actively participates in a process.

### 3. Preserve the scientific account

Every investigation retains the scientific account alongside the GUA-W description:

1. **Scientific subject:** Name the phenomenon and discipline using established terminology.
2. **Observation:** State what was observed or measured, by whom, and under what conditions.
3. **Evidence and method:** Identify the data, experimental design, instruments, sample, model, or observational method.
4. **Scientific explanation:** Present the proposed mechanism in the discipline's own language.
5. **Confidence and limits:** Separate established findings, supported interpretations, hypotheses, uncertainty, and open disputes.
6. **GUA-W description:** Ask whether the foundational vocabulary clarifies the observed relationships without replacing the scientific account.
7. **Comparison:** Identify where the same mechanism is present, where only a similar relationship appears, and where the resemblance is merely visual or verbal.
8. **Unresolved questions:** Preserve what the evidence does not yet establish.

A GUA-W description is not itself evidence for a scientific mechanism. A scientific mechanism is not automatically a theological conclusion.

### 4. Questions to apply during investigation

Use these as investigative questions, not as a forced checklist whose every category must receive an answer:

- What entities, components, or conditions are involved?
- What is arranged in relation to what?
- What supports or establishes that arrangement?
- What recurring pattern is actually observed?
- What constraints or boundary conditions affect the phenomenon?
- What changes when those conditions change?
- What function is demonstrated, and what purpose—if any—is inferred?
- What becomes manifest or measurable?
- What registers the result, and what are the limits of that witness?
- Are we witnessing the same mechanism, a similar relationship, or merely a similar appearance?
- Does a boundary merely separate two regions, or can it also participate in the relationship between them?

### 5. Evidence discipline

Keep these distinctions explicit:

- **Observation:** What the evidence directly records.
- **Measurement:** A quantified result obtained through a stated method.
- **Mechanism:** A causal process supported by evidence.
- **Model:** A representation used to explain, calculate, or predict.
- **Interpretation:** Meaning assigned to observations or results.
- **Hypothesis:** A testable proposed explanation.
- **Analogy:** A comparison that may illuminate a relationship but does not establish shared mechanism.
- **Unknown:** A question not resolved by available evidence.

Do not promote an analogy into a mechanism, a correlation into causation, a model into a direct observation, or a functional description into proof of ultimate purpose.

When sources disagree, record the disagreement and its basis rather than manufacturing consensus. Prefer primary research for specific experimental claims and use reviews to map the broader state of a field. Record enough bibliographic detail for another reader to locate and check the source.

### 6. Cross-disciplinary consistency

The same vocabulary may be useful across physics, chemistry, biology, astronomy, geology, ecology, and other fields. Its meaning must remain precise enough to avoid flattening real differences.

For each proposed cross-disciplinary connection, state whether it is:

- the same identified mechanism;
- a different mechanism with a comparable relationship;
- a limited analogy;
- or an unsupported resemblance that should not be carried forward.

A shared word does not prove a shared process. Similar patterns do not establish common causation. Differences are findings and must be preserved.

**Common descriptive grammar principle:** Use the shared vocabulary as a disciplined means of asking comparable questions across domains—not as a claim that the domains share one mechanism. The grammar may help identify components, relationships, conditions, constraints, operations, manifestations, and witnesses. Each field retains its own definitions, evidence, scales, and causal explanations. Let the observed phenomenon determine which terms are useful; leave a term unused when it does not clarify the evidence. The purpose of comparison is to discover what is genuinely common, what is only relationally comparable, and what remains distinct or unresolved.

### 7. Relationship to Genesis 1–2 and GUA-W

Genesis 1–2 KJV is the scriptural origin of the foundational vocabulary used in this project. The repository investigates scientific observations through that vocabulary while keeping distinct what the biblical text says, what science observes, and what the investigator proposes as a relationship between them.

Do not force a scientific result into a scriptural passage. Do not use a scientific result to rewrite the passage. Do not claim that scientific coherence is scientific confirmation of Scripture. Preserve the distinction between the originating Source, the created order, and derivative witnesses that observe or describe it.

### 8. A repeatable investigation record

Each new investigation should contain:

1. Title and scientific discipline(s).
2. The question that initiated the investigation.
3. Why the question matters scientifically.
4. Relevant scientific terminology and definitions.
5. Observations, methods, and evidence.
6. Current scientific explanations and competing interpretations.
7. A careful GUA-W description using only terms that genuinely clarify the evidence.
8. A relationship audit: same mechanism, comparable relationship, analogy, or unsupported resemblance.
9. Boundaries, constraints, and conditions that affect the result.
10. What is established, what is inferred, and what remains unknown.
11. Sources.
12. The next open question.

### 10. Apply the Four-Question Study Method

The [Four-Question Study Method](FOUR-QUESTION-STUDY-METHOD.md) is a companion discipline to this research method. Use its four questions recursively during substantial investigations:

1. **What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?** Examine the inquiry, assumptions, distinctions, and evidence required.
2. **What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?** Return to the established scriptural foundation and relevant source records; identify the basis and limits of any proposed correspondence.
3. **What am I learning about what I am witnessing?** Examine the scientific subject, observations, mechanisms, competing explanations, and unknowns.
4. **What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?** Examine the investigator's position, assumptions, methods, dependence, and limitations.

The questions work together; they are not four sequential research stages or an additional GUA-W architectural layer. Include a concise reflection under each when useful to the investigation. Do not force a conclusion when evidence leaves a question unresolved.

The companion method also carries two recurring comparison questions into each relevant investigation:

- **Are we witnessing the same mechanism, a similar relationship, or merely a similar appearance?**
- **Does a boundary merely separate two regions, or can it also participate in the relationship between them?**

When relevant, explicitly classify the comparison and identify evidence for the boundary's role. These questions refine the examination; they do not predetermine its result.

### 11. Starting principle

Begin with the phenomenon, not with a predetermined answer. Let observations determine which vocabulary is useful. Allow a term to remain unused when it does not clarify the evidence. Revise a working description when it obscures distinctions. Preserve scientific explanations in their own language.

The aim is a consistent language of description across the sciences—not a substitute for scientific investigation.

**One Author. One Creation. Many witnesses.**
