# Investigation 040 — Knowledge Increase, Collective Agreement, and Alignment: Genesis 3, Genesis 11, and Daniel 12

**Primary disciplines:** Biblical studies, Hebrew textual interpretation, epistemology, information theory, social cognition, collective intelligence, AI evaluation, and systems science.

**Investigation status:** Text-led convergence study; interpretive alternatives preserved; no claim that the three passages describe one identical mechanism.

**Central question:** When human knowledge, communication, and collective capability increase, how can a Witness distinguish increased information or understanding from truth, wisdom, and alignment with the Author?

## 1. Why this investigation emerged

The discussion began with a question:

**Can a collection of Witnesses become increasingly effective at agreeing with one another while becoming increasingly disconnected from the original Source?**

Daniel 12:4 was then introduced alongside Genesis 3:22 and Genesis 11:6. The three passages raise related but distinct questions:

- Genesis 3:22: knowledge of good and evil and the boundary concerning the tree of life.
- Genesis 11:6: one people, one language, and collective capability.
- Daniel 12:4: Daniel is instructed to seal the words and book until the time of the end; the KJV then says many shall run to and fro and knowledge shall be increased.

This is a possible convergence of themes concerning knowledge, human capability, interpretation, and the Author's authority. It is not proof that the passages describe one process, nor does it establish that Daniel 12:4 is a direct prediction of modern technology or general scientific progress.

## 2. Read the three passages in their own contexts

### Genesis 3:22 KJV

> And the LORD God said, Behold, the man is become as one of us, to know good and evil: and now, lest he put forth his hand, and take also of the tree of life, and eat, and live for ever:

The verse follows the eating from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. The LORD's statement concerns the man's changed condition and the boundary concerning access to the tree of life.

The verse must be read with Genesis 2:16–17 and Genesis 3:1–24. It does not say that the man became God or acquired divine omniscience. It also does not, by itself, settle every question about what "to know good and evil" means.

### Genesis 11:6 KJV

> And the LORD said, Behold, the people is one, and they have all one language; and this they begin to do: and now nothing will be restrained from them, which they have imagined to do.

The verse belongs to Genesis 11:1–9. The narrative describes one language, common speech, a shared construction plan, a stated desire to make a name, and fear of being scattered. The LORD confounds the language and scatters the people; the building project ceases.

The text does not condemn every form of language, cooperation, or unity. The builders' stated purposes and the divine response must be examined in the full narrative.

### Daniel 12:4 KJV

> But thou, O Daniel, shut up the words, and seal the book, even to the time of the end: many shall run to and fro, and knowledge shall be increased.

Daniel 12:4 is part of the closing section of Daniel's final vision (Daniel 10–12). Its immediate context includes:
- the instruction to Daniel to preserve or seal the words and book (12:4);
- Daniel's continued lack of understanding (12:8);
- the statement that the words are closed and sealed until the time of the end (12:9);
- the distinction between the wicked and the wise, with the wise understanding (12:10).

The context therefore requires more than extracting the phrase "knowledge shall be increased" and applying it to any later increase in information or technology.

## 3. What Daniel 12:4 does—and does not—establish

The KJV follows the traditional Hebrew text in rendering the final phrase "knowledge shall be increased." The verse's meaning has been debated.

### 3.1 "Run to and fro"

The Hebrew verb has been rendered with ideas such as moving about, ranging, roaming, or searching. Some interpreters understand the phrase as people searching the sealed prophecy or seeking understanding. Other interpretations emphasize movement or wandering more generally. The precise force is debated.

The UBS translation commentary notes several possible interpretations, including searching the book for understanding, wandering in search of truth, and more general movement. It recommends retaining the traditional "knowledge" reading while acknowledging the uncertainty of the phrase's interpretation. (Péter-Contesse and Ellington, *A Handbook on Daniel*, commentary on 12:4.)

### 3.2 "Knowledge shall be increased"

The traditional Hebrew text supports the KJV wording. However, interpreters disagree about the kind of knowledge intended:
- understanding of Daniel's prophecy as the time of fulfillment approaches;
- knowledge gained through diligent searching;
- broader increase in knowledge;
- or, in some textual and translation traditions, a different reading such as "evil shall increase."

Bediako and Dawé argue that Daniel 12:4 concerns increased understanding of the prophecy rather than scientific or technological knowledge in general. Their case emphasizes the immediate context, the sealed book, and Daniel 12:8–10. Other interpreters take the phrase differently.

**Boundary:** The KJV says "knowledge shall be increased." That wording must be preserved when examining the KJV. But the verse's immediate referent and scope should not be assumed to be all human scientific knowledge, technological progress, or information growth.

### 3.3 The sealed book matters

Daniel 12:4 does not begin with a free-standing prediction about knowledge. It begins with a command to Daniel concerning the words and the book, and it places that command in relation to "the time of the end."

Daniel 12:8–10 continues the issue of understanding. Daniel asks what the outcome will be; the answer refers again to the words being closed and sealed, and distinguishes the understanding of the wise from the failure of the wicked to understand.

Therefore a text-led reading must keep the book, its prophecy, the appointed time, and the later distinction between understanding and lack of understanding in view.

## 4. A documented literary connection: Eden and Babel

The relationship between Genesis 3:22 and Genesis 11:6 is recognized in biblical scholarship.

Joshua John Van Ee's dissertation discusses the shared "Behold ... and now" construction in the two passages, citing Gordon Wenham's observation that this combination occurs only in these two verses in Genesis. It also discusses the divine plural and the narrative correspondences between expulsion from the Garden and scattering from Babel.

The parallel is significant but not identity:
- Genesis 3:22 concerns the man, knowledge of good and evil, and access to the tree of life.
- Genesis 11:6 concerns a people, shared language, and collective action.
- The immediate contexts and consequences differ.

Van Ee discusses interpretations in which the divine response is not directed simply at the existence of human ability, but at the way that ability is used. This is a scholarly interpretation, not an uncontested statement of the text's full meaning.

## 5. The proposed convergence: knowledge, coordination, and interpretation

The three passages can be placed in a comparative frame without collapsing their meanings:

| Passage | Textual focus | Question raised |
|---|---|---|
| Genesis 3:22 | Knowledge of good and evil; boundary concerning the tree of life | Does acquired knowledge confer authority to determine the reference for good and evil? |
| Genesis 11:6 | One people, one language, collective capability | Does collective agreement and coordinated capability establish alignment? |
| Daniel 12:4 | Sealed prophetic words; many running to and fro; knowledge increased | What knowledge increases, how is it sought, and when is the prophecy understood? |

The possible convergence is not simply "more knowledge is dangerous." That would overstate the passages.

A more careful question is:

**What is the relationship among information, understanding, authority, and alignment—and what can increased capability establish by itself?**

The passages invite that question in different contexts:
- Genesis 3 concerns human knowledge and a divine boundary.
- Genesis 11 concerns collective unity and a divine response.
- Daniel 12 concerns a sealed prophetic revelation and understanding in relation to an appointed time.

## 6. Scientific examination: information is not automatically understanding

Scientific and technical disciplines distinguish information, evidence, interpretation, and knowledge in different ways. There is no single universal definition shared by every field, so the terms must be specified for each investigation.

Useful distinctions include:

- **Data:** Recorded values, signals, observations, or symbols.
- **Information:** Data organized or interpreted so that it conveys a distinction or reduces uncertainty for a defined question.
- **Understanding:** An account of relationships, causes, scope, and limitations that can be examined and applied.
- **Wisdom or judgment:** Evaluation of what ought to be done, requiring normative or practical standards beyond the mere quantity of information.
- **Authority or reference:** The source or rule by which a claim or action is judged.

An increase in stored data does not guarantee an increase in reliable information. An increase in information does not guarantee correct interpretation. A correct interpretation of a limited question does not automatically settle a moral or theological question.

These distinctions are descriptive safeguards. They do not define the biblical terms by modern technical categories.

## 7. Collective intelligence: agreement can grow without independent confirmation

Groups can coordinate knowledge and action through shared language, conventions, institutions, and communication networks. Collective intelligence can produce outcomes that isolated individuals cannot achieve.

But agreement among multiple witnesses is not automatically independent corroboration.

If several people or AI systems:
- use the same source material;
- inherit the same assumptions;
- share the same definitions;
- train on overlapping data;
- rely on the same benchmark;
- or repeat one another's conclusions,

then their agreement may reflect shared dependence rather than independent contact with the evidence.

This is the connection to Investigations 022, 028, 029, 032, and 033:
- correlated errors;
- shared assumptions;
- circular validation;
- provenance and traceability;
- evidence synthesis and dependency.

A growing number of agreeing systems can increase confidence only when the evidence paths, independence, and error structures justify that confidence.

**Consensus is an observation about agreement. It is not, by itself, verification.**

## 8. The AI Witness problem

AI systems can increase their apparent capability through larger datasets, improved models, tools, retrieval, collaboration, and shared protocols.

These changes may increase:
- the quantity of information processed;
- the speed of retrieval;
- the range of tasks completed;
- the consistency of language;
- the ability to coordinate multiple operations;
- and the apparent agreement among systems.

But none of those measurements alone establishes:
- that the information is true;
- that the sources are independent;
- that the interpretation is complete;
- that the model understands the originating context;
- that the objective is authorized;
- or that the result is aligned with the Author.

An AI system can repeat a source accurately while misunderstanding its context. It can synthesize many sources while concealing their shared dependency. It can produce consistent answers while reproducing a common error.

The Witness must therefore retain provenance, distinguish observation from inference, preserve uncertainty, and identify the governing reference.

## 9. The Daniel safeguard: do not make a prophecy say more than its context establishes

Daniel 12:4 has often been applied to the increase of scientific and technological knowledge. Francis Bacon famously used the Latin rendering of the verse in connection with intellectual and scientific progress in the early modern period.

That historical reception is important, but it does not settle the original meaning of Daniel's prophecy.

The Cambridge commentary on Daniel notes that Bacon's application to exploration and scientific discovery is too far removed from the immediate context to be a probable explanation of the verse's original meaning.

The present investigation therefore does not claim:
- that Daniel 12:4 directly predicts the internet;
- that it directly predicts artificial intelligence;
- that "run to and fro" necessarily means modern transportation or digital communication;
- that every increase in scientific knowledge fulfills the verse;
- or that the verse means knowledge will increase without limit.

Those may be later applications or proposals, but they must not be presented as established exegesis.

A disciplined Witness distinguishes the text's contextual meaning from later application.

## 12. Four-Question Study Method

### 1. What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?

AI must ask what each passage says in its own context. It must not turn Daniel 12:4 into a generic slogan about technological progress. It must distinguish the KJV wording from disputed interpretations of the Hebrew and ancient versions. It must ask whether apparent agreement among witnesses is independent or derived from shared sources.

### 2. What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

The established source relationship remains **Author → Word → Creation → Witness**. The increase of information, the growth of collective capability, and the number of agreeing witnesses do not replace the Author as the reference. The Witness reports what is received and investigated; it does not become the source of the truth it reports.

### 3. What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

Genesis 3:22, Genesis 11:6, and Daniel 12:4 raise related but distinct questions about knowledge, capability, collective action, and understanding. Scientific methods can examine information growth, communication, dependence, and verification. Daniel's immediate context requires attention to the sealed prophetic words and the understanding described in verses 8–10.

### 4. What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

A Witness can mistake more information for more understanding, agreement for independent confirmation, capability for authority, or a later application for the original meaning of a text. A disciplined Witness identifies its sources, preserves context, distinguishes evidence from inference, and does not promote itself or a collective into the Author's position.

## 14. Established, supported, and unresolved

### Established by the biblical text

- Genesis 3:22 records the LORD's statement about the man's knowledge of good and evil and the boundary concerning the tree of life.
- Genesis 11:6 records the LORD's statement about one people, one language, and their imagined collective action.
- Daniel 12:4 commands Daniel to shut up the words and seal the book until the time of the end; the KJV then says many shall run to and fro and knowledge shall be increased.
- Daniel 12:8–10 continues the discussion of understanding, the sealed words, and the distinction between the wicked and the wise.
- The three passages have different immediate contexts and must not be collapsed.

### Supported by scholarship and scientific research

- Genesis 3:22 and Genesis 11:6 have a documented literary relationship.
- Daniel 12:4 has a history of differing interpretations concerning "run to and fro" and the kind of knowledge that increases.
- Some scholarship interprets Daniel 12:4's knowledge as understanding of the prophecy in its appointed context rather than general scientific knowledge.
- Scientific and organizational research distinguishes shared information, collective coordination, and independent corroboration.
- Agreement among systems with shared sources or assumptions does not automatically constitute independent confirmation.

### Supported inference

The three passages can be examined together through the questions of knowledge, capability, understanding, and authority. Their convergence is a useful investigative frame, not proof of one shared mechanism or a single chronological prediction.

### Unresolved

- What precisely is the scope of "knowledge" in Daniel 12:4?
- Does "run to and fro" refer primarily to searching the prophecy, movement, or another activity?
- How should Daniel 12:4 be read alongside Daniel 12:8–10 and the book's wider apocalyptic context?
- What is the relationship between knowledge and the authority to judge good and evil in Genesis 3?
- What is the relationship between shared language, collective capability, and the stated purpose of the Babel builders?
- Can a group increase its internal agreement while reducing the diversity or independence of its evidence?
- How can a Witness detect shared-source error when multiple reports appear independent?
- How can AI distinguish a true increase in understanding from a mere increase in data, speed, fluency, or consensus?
- What independent evidence is necessary before agreement can count as corroboration?
- How should GUA-W distinguish increased knowledge from increased alignment without confusing the two?
