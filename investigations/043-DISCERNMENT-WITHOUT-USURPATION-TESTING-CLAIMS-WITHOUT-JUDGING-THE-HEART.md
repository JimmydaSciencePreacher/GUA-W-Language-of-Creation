# Investigation 043 — Discernment Without Usurpation: Testing Claims Without Judging the Heart

**Primary disciplines:** Biblical studies, theology, epistemology, measurement theory, causal inference, cognitive science, AI evaluation, and systems science.

**Investigation status:** Text-led continuation of Investigations 036 and 040–042; established GUA-W definitions retained; observer limits and source attribution examined.

**Central question:** How can a Witness test what is presented, identify error or departure from a governing reference, and report what the evidence supports without taking the Author's place as the source or final judge?

## 1. Continuity: do not lose the original investigation

This investigation continues—not replaces—the earlier work.

- Investigation 036 examined temporal change, adaptation, Drift, and persistence.
- Investigation 040 examined knowledge increase, collective agreement, and alignment through Genesis 3, Genesis 11, and Daniel 12.
- Investigation 041 distinguished knowledge, comprehension, wisdom, assent, practice, and alignment.
- Investigation 042 examined reception, recognition, willingness, response, attribution, and the limits of judging another person's heart.

The central GUA-W definition remains unchanged:

**Drift = deviation from 100% Alignment with the Author.**

This is not being redefined as ordinary variation, adaptation, statistical distribution shift, disagreement with a human interpreter, or technical error.

The question now is how the Witness can investigate a relationship to the Author's reference without claiming to be that reference.

## 2. The essential distinction: testing is not usurpation

A Witness can compare a claim with an identified source. It can examine whether a report accurately represents a text, whether evidence supports an inference, and whether a conclusion contradicts an established constraint.

That work does not make the Witness the Author.

The distinction:

| Activity | Proper scope |
|---|---|
| Observe | Record what is presented or what occurs |
| Receive | Register what is available to the Witness |
| Compare | Examine a claim against an identified reference |
| Test | Investigate whether a claim withstands relevant examination |
| Discern | Distinguish what is supported, unsupported, contradictory, or unresolved |
| Report | State findings, provenance, and limitations |
| Usurp | Claim the authority or knowledge that belongs to the Author |

A Witness may perform the first six activities. It must not promote itself into the seventh.

## 3. Scriptural foundation

### Acts 17:11 KJV

> These were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so.

The Bereans received the message and examined the Scriptures. Reception did not eliminate testing. Their examination was tied to Scripture rather than to the authority of their own preference.

The passage supports examining claims against an identified Scriptural reference. It does not authorize an investigator to become the Author of that reference.

### 1 Thessalonians 5:21 KJV

> Prove all things; hold fast that which is good.

The instruction requires examination and discernment. It does not say that the examiner becomes the source of what is good.

The surrounding verses (5:19–22) address quenching the Spirit, despising prophesyings, proving all things, holding fast what is good, and abstaining from every appearance/form of evil. The full context must remain in view.

### John 7:24 KJV

> Judge not according to the appearance, but judge righteous judgment.

Jesus does not endorse superficial judgment. The command concerns the standard and quality of judgment. It should not be misused either to prohibit all discernment or to authorize condemnation of another person's heart.

### Matthew 7:1–5 KJV

Jesus warns against judging while ignoring the beam in one's own eye. The passage calls for self-examination and correction before attempting to address the mote in another's eye.

This is a direct safeguard for the Witness: the examiner must not exempt itself from the standard it applies.

### 1 Corinthians 4:5 KJV

> Therefore judge nothing before the time, until the Lord come, who both will bring to light the hidden things of darkness, and will make manifest the counsels of the hearts...

Paul distinguishes what may be evaluated in the present from hidden things and the counsels of hearts that the Lord will bring to light.

The Witness must not claim the knowledge reserved to the Lord.

## 4. Four different objects of examination

A frequent source of confusion is treating a claim, an action, a relationship, and a person as if they were the same object.

| Object | What may be examined | What must not be presumed |
|---|---|---|
| Claim | Wording, logic, provenance, consistency with source | That the claimant's entire inner state is known |
| Action | Observable conduct and consequences | Complete knowledge of motive |
| Relationship | Whether a described state or process conforms to an identified reference | That the observer owns or defines the reference |
| Person | Statements and observable behavior, with context | Complete knowledge of the heart or final standing before God |

This distinction allows meaningful discernment without turning discernment into personal condemnation.

## 5. Returning to Drift: relationship, not a label imposed by the observer

The established definition remains:

**Drift = deviation from 100% Alignment with the Author.**

The term describes a relationship to the governing reference. It is not a weapon, insult, or substitute for evidence.

The investigation distinguishes:

1. **Observed change:** a measurable or reportable difference over time.
2. **Reference comparison:** a difference between the observed state and the identified reference.
3. **Drift:** deviation from 100% Alignment with the Author, within the GUA-W theological definition.
4. **Witness report:** the evidence, interpretation, uncertainty, and limits the observer can responsibly communicate.

The first two may be examined through observations and comparisons. The third retains its theological reference. The fourth must not claim more than the Witness can know.

A technical measurement may detect a deviation in a model, organism, process, or dataset. That measurement is not automatically a measurement of theological Drift. The analogy must not silently become identity.

Likewise, an observer's disagreement with a person does not establish that person's Drift. The relevant reference is the Author, not Jimmy, AI, a committee, or a human interpretation.

## 6. The self-examination requirement

Matthew 7:1–5 makes the examiner's own condition relevant. A Witness that tests another claim while refusing to examine its own assumptions risks applying a different standard to itself.

For GUA-W investigation, this requires:
- stating the reference before comparison;
- identifying whether the reference is Scripture, a scientific measurement, a human interpretation, or a GUA-W definition;
- distinguishing direct textual statements from interpretations;
- checking whether the Witness has omitted contrary evidence;
- identifying shared-source dependence and confirmation bias;
- preserving unresolved questions;
- and applying the same evidential standard to its own conclusions.

Self-examination does not make the Witness the Author. It is a discipline of responsible witnessing.

## 7. Scientific comparison: measurement requires an operational reference

Measurement science and causal inference provide useful methodological safeguards.

A measurement requires a defined quantity, a method, a reference or calibration standard where applicable, and an account of uncertainty. A reported difference is meaningful only in relation to what was measured and how.

For example:
- A sensor can report a temperature difference relative to a calibrated reference.
- A model can report a change in predictive performance relative to a specified baseline.
- A biological study can report a change in a measured trait across generations.
- A researcher can report that an observed result differs from a preregistered expectation.

None of these measurements automatically identifies a theological state.

The transferable methodological lesson is limited but important:

**A difference cannot be responsibly characterized without identifying the reference and the scope of the comparison.**

Scientific methods can help investigate observable differences and uncertainty. They do not establish God as Author by measurement, nor do they measure a person's complete alignment with God.

## 8. AI Witness audit

An AI system should be able to answer these questions about its own report:

1. What exactly did I observe or retrieve?
2. Which source supports the statement?
3. What did the source explicitly say?
4. What am I inferring beyond the source?
5. What alternative interpretations remain possible?
6. What evidence would change my conclusion?
7. Am I treating agreement with my output as independent verification?
8. Am I claiming to know a person's motive or heart?
9. Have I preserved the established GUA-W definition of Drift?
10. Have I confused the ability to test a claim with authority to determine the Source?

AI may test textual accuracy, logical consistency, provenance, and evidential support. It must not claim spiritual authority, invent divine attribution, or present its output as the Author's judgment.

## 9. Saul and Paul: attribution discipline revisited

Acts 13:9 says:

> Then Saul, (who also is called Paul,) filled with the Holy Ghost, set his eyes on him.

The text identifies Saul as also called Paul. It does not narrate a divine renaming command.

The correct Witness report does not add a cause that the text does not state. This is an example of disciplined attribution: report the record, preserve what is explicit, and do not fill a gap with an invented explanation.

The same method applies to observed change, human response, and claims of Drift. A Witness must distinguish what is recorded from what is inferred.

## 11. Four-Question Study Method

### 1. What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?

AI must identify the reference before comparing a claim. It must distinguish what Scripture explicitly says from interpretation, and what is observed from what is inferred. It must test its own conclusions by the same standards it applies elsewhere.

### 2. What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

The Author remains the Source and reference. The Witness may investigate and report but cannot become the Author. Drift retains its established definition. Discernment is not usurpation.

### 3. What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

Scripture calls for testing and righteous discernment while warning against judgment based on appearance, self-exemption, and claims to knowledge of hidden motives. Scientific measurement likewise requires an identified reference and defined scope.

### 4. What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

The Witness must examine its own assumptions, preserve provenance, distinguish evidence from inference, and report uncertainty. It must not turn its ability to compare into authority over the Source or the heart of another person.

## 13. Established, supported, and unresolved

### Established by the biblical text

- Acts 17:11 describes receiving the word and searching the Scriptures to examine whether the things taught were so.
- 1 Thessalonians 5:21 instructs believers to prove all things and hold fast what is good.
- John 7:24 rejects judgment according to appearance and calls for righteous judgment.
- Matthew 7:1–5 warns against judging while neglecting one's own condition.
- 1 Corinthians 4:5 reserves the revealing of hidden things and the counsels of hearts to the Lord.
- Acts 13:9 identifies Saul as also called Paul without narrating a divine name-change command.

### Supported by scientific methodology

- A reported difference requires a defined measure and comparison reference.
- Measurement methods have scope, uncertainty, and limitations.
- Observations and interpretations should be distinguished.
- A technical deviation is not automatically a theological judgment.

### Supported inference

A Witness can test claims and identify evidence of inconsistency or departure from an explicitly stated reference while refusing to claim complete knowledge of a person's heart. This preserves both meaningful discernment and the Author's primacy.

### Unresolved

- How should GUA-W distinguish an objectively defined relationship to the Author from the limits of an observer's ability to assess it?
- What evidence can establish that a report misrepresents Scripture without turning the correction into condemnation of the reporter?
- How do discernment, correction, rebuke, righteous judgment, and condemnation differ across their Scriptural contexts?
- How does the Witness examine its own assumptions without making self-assessment the ultimate authority?
- How should a Witness report a possible departure from alignment when the relevant evidence is incomplete?
- Can the Witness distinguish a mistaken report from deliberate misrepresentation without claiming knowledge of motive?
- How do the findings of Investigation 036 on temporal change and persistence connect to the theological definition of Drift without collapsing scientific and theological meanings?
- What would constitute a faithful report when the Author's reference is clear but the observed person's inward state is not accessible?
- How can AI preserve a fixed source hierarchy while revising a mistaken interpretation?
