# Investigation 045 — Collective Agreement, Knowledge, and Alignment: Human Determination and “I Never Knew You”

**Primary disciplines:** Biblical studies, theology, Hebrew and Greek textual interpretation, epistemology, social cognition, collective intelligence, information theory, causal inference, AI evaluation, and systems science.

**Investigation status:** Initial text-led investigation. Continues Investigations 040–044. The established GUA-W definition of Drift is preserved: deviation from 100% Alignment with the Author. This investigation does not judge individual hearts or assign spiritual status.

## 1. Central question

> Can many Witnesses become increasingly knowledgeable, increasingly coordinated, and increasingly convinced of their agreement, while the originating reference remains unchanged and their relationship to it departs from Alignment?

**Working answer:** Yes, this is a coherent possibility. Collective knowledge, coordination, confidence, and agreement are properties of the collective's information and relationships with one another. They do not, by themselves, establish fidelity to an originating reference outside that collective.

The question is not whether agreement is always wrong. Agreement may accurately reflect a source. The question is whether agreement is being treated as evidence of alignment without independently examining the reference to which alignment is claimed.

The originating reference need not change for the Witnesses' relationship to it to change.

## 2. The user's proposed theological connection

The user identifies two related lines of inquiry:

1. Modern theological use of **“Free Will”**, understood in this investigation as the elevation of human will, determination, or choice into an independent reference for deciding what is good, true, or authoritative.
2. Christ's words in **Matthew 7:23 KJV**: “I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity.”

These are proposed interpretive connections to examine, not conclusions to impose on every theological tradition or every person who uses the phrase *free will*.

A necessary distinction: Christian traditions do not all define free will in the same way. Some use it to discuss voluntary action, moral responsibility, or the capacity to respond; others make stronger claims about the independence or decisive priority of human choice. These meanings must not be collapsed. The investigation concerns the particular claim that human determination becomes an independent authority or originating reference—not the mere recognition that people make choices.

**Research distinction:** A human will can be a real feature of human action without becoming the Author, the originating cause, or the final standard of truth.

## 3. Biblical examination: the knowledge-and-determination sequence

### 3.1 Genesis 3:1–7 KJV — the question of reference

Genesis 3 records a sequence involving the serpent's words, Eve's reception and consideration, her seeing, her evaluation, her desire, and the act of taking and eating.

Genesis 3:6 describes the tree as:
- good for food;
- pleasant to the eyes;
- desired to make one wise.

The text records an evaluation and a response. It does not say that Adam and Eve were appointed as the originating authority over good and evil. The command already existed before the temptation (Genesis 2:16–17).

**Textual observation:** The command is prior to the human evaluation recorded in Genesis 3:6.

**Interpretive question:** Does the temptation invite the human Witness to treat its own evaluation as sufficient authority to determine what is good and desirable, apart from the Word already given?

This is a bounded theological reading. It must not be turned into the claim that all evaluation, desire, choice, or human agency is itself sinful. The issue is the reference and authority by which a determination is made.

Genesis 3:22 must also remain in context. The Lord says that man has become “as one of us, to know good and evil.” The verse does not authorize the Witness to become the standard by which good and evil are ultimately defined. The full passage, including Genesis 3:22–24, must be read rather than isolated.

### 3.2 Genesis 11:1–9 KJV — collective unity and capability

Genesis 11:6 records the Lord's observation that the people are one and have one language, and that what they have imagined to do will not be restrained from them.

**Textual observation:** Shared language and unity enable coordinated collective action.

The passage does not identify agreement as proof of alignment with God. Nor does it teach that every form of collective cooperation is wrong. Its context is the people's project at Babel and God's response to it.

**Investigative distinction:** Coordination answers how effectively a group can act together. Alignment asks what reference governs the group's relationship and action. These are not interchangeable questions.

### 3.3 Daniel 12:4 and 12:10 — knowledge and understanding

Daniel 12:4 speaks of the words being sealed and of knowledge increasing. Its immediate context is Daniel's prophetic revelation and the appointed horizon of its understanding. The verse should not be presented as an unambiguous prediction of modern information technology.

Daniel 12:10 distinguishes the wicked who do not understand from the wise who understand, within the chapter's prophetic and judgment context.

These verses keep knowledge, understanding, and wisdom distinct. They do not authorize an observer to label everyone who disagrees with that observer as wicked.

### 3.4 Proverbs 1:7 — the reference for knowledge

Proverbs 1:7 KJV states: “The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but fools despise wisdom and instruction.”

This verse is directly relevant to the proposed distinction. Knowledge is not presented as self-originating or as an authority independent of the Lord. Its beginning is located in a relationship to the Lord.

The verse does not make learning, investigation, or the accumulation of information suspect. It identifies the governing orientation within which knowledge is rightly understood.

## 4. “I never knew you” — Matthew 7:21–23

Matthew 7:21–23 must be read as a unit. Jesus contrasts merely saying “Lord, Lord” with doing the will of His Father. The passage then describes people who appeal to impressive works, including prophecy, casting out devils, and many wonderful works. Christ's response is that He never knew them and identifies them as workers of iniquity.

**Textual observations:**
- A verbal profession is present.
- Impressive works are claimed.
- The claimants appeal to those works.
- Christ, not the claimants or the crowd, supplies the decisive assessment.
- The passage connects entry into the kingdom with doing the Father's will (Matthew 7:21).

This is not a statement that Christ lacks information about these people. In the passage, “I never knew you” is relational and authoritative language, not a measure of how much information the claimants possessed about Jesus.

The passage therefore raises a question central to this investigation:

> Can a person or collective possess religious vocabulary, extensive information, coordinated activity, and strong confidence in its own standing, while those features do not establish the relationship Christ identifies as decisive?

The passage supports the distinction between outward profession or claimed accomplishment and the relationship recognized by Christ. It does **not** permit a Witness to announce that a named individual is among those addressed by the warning. Christ speaks as Judge; the derivative Witness reports His words and examines claims without usurping His authority.

## 5. The collective-knowledge problem: agreement is not independent confirmation

The scientific and epistemic comparison begins with a familiar problem: multiple reports may appear to be independent while sharing the same upstream source.

Examples include:
- many publications repeating one original report;
- several analysts using the same dataset and assumptions;
- multiple AI systems trained on overlapping material;
- a committee whose members rely on the same authority or interpretive framework;
- repeated summaries that all descend from one unverified claim.

The number of reports is not the same as the number of independent evidentiary sources.

If ten Witnesses repeat one upstream report, the collective has ten expressions but may have only one underlying evidentiary origin. Their agreement may demonstrate successful transmission and coordination, not ten independent confirmations.

This is a problem of **correlated evidence and shared provenance**, investigated earlier in this repository's work on measurement independence, evidence fusion, provenance, and evidence synthesis.

A disciplined Witness therefore asks:
1. What is the original source?
2. Which reports are genuinely independent?
3. Which reports share data, assumptions, training material, authorities, or interpretive steps?
4. Has the originating record been checked directly?
5. What evidence could distinguish accurate agreement from coordinated repetition?
6. What contrary evidence or alternative interpretation has not been considered?

A consensus can be well supported. It can also be dependent, circular, or mistaken. The fact of consensus alone does not decide which.

## 6. Knowledge, coordination, confidence, and Alignment are different variables

| Dimension | What it describes | What it does not establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge | Information received, retained, or communicated | Wisdom or Alignment |
| Understanding | Recognition of meaning and relationships | That the recognized reference is accepted |
| Coordination | Ability to organize action across Witnesses | Fidelity to the Author |
| Agreement | Convergence of expressed conclusions | Independent confirmation or truth |
| Confidence | Strength of conviction | Accuracy or source fidelity |
| Capability | What a collective can accomplish | Whether its purpose or governing reference is aligned |
| Alignment | Relationship to the Author | A status that a human observer can fully measure in another person's heart |

The dimensions can increase together, decrease together, or move in different directions. A collective may become more capable and more internally consistent while becoming less responsive to correction from its originating reference.

This is a conceptual possibility, not a claim that every coordinated group is misaligned.

## 7. The proposed “Free Will” connection: identify the exact claim

The phrase *free will* is used for different theological and philosophical claims. A fair investigation must identify which meaning is under examination before evaluating it.

For this investigation, distinguish:

**A. Voluntary agency:** People make choices and act willingly. This description does not by itself make human beings the originating cause or final authority.

**B. Moral responsibility:** People are addressed as responsible for their actions. This question must be examined across the full Scriptural context rather than removed from passages concerning God's initiative and sovereignty.

**C. Independent moral determination:** Human will is treated as the ultimate authority for determining good and evil, apart from or over the originating Word. This is the specific claim under examination.

The GUA-W question is not simply, “Does man have a will?” It is:

> Is the human will a derivative capacity operating within Creation, or is it being treated as an independent originating authority that can establish its own reference?

The distinction preserves human action without elevating human determination into the Author's position.

A theological survey must not imply that every advocate of free will teaches category C. Some traditions explicitly deny that human choice is independent of God. Others use the phrase to defend voluntary action or responsibility. Their actual claims must be examined, not inferred from a label.

## 8. Scientific and systems comparison: collective convergence under shared assumptions

Several established fields provide useful, bounded comparisons:

### 8.1 Correlated observations and evidence fusion

When observations share upstream data or systematic error, combining them as though independent can overstate evidential strength. The collective result may appear more certain than the underlying evidence warrants.

**GUA-W comparison:** Many Witnesses can report a common conclusion without supplying many independent paths to the originating record.

**Classification:** Comparable epistemic relationship; not an identical theological mechanism.

### 8.2 Social influence and information cascades

In social decision-making, individuals may rely on earlier choices or public signals rather than independently evaluating all available evidence. A cascade can produce widespread agreement even when early information was incomplete or mistaken.

This does not mean social learning is inherently defective. Learning from others is often useful. The question is whether later Witnesses retain access to the underlying evidence and the ability to revise a conclusion when stronger evidence appears.

**GUA-W comparison:** Agreement may become self-reinforcing when the group's agreement is treated as the evidence for the agreement.

**Classification:** Comparable relationship; the social mechanism must be demonstrated in each case.

### 8.3 Common-mode failure

In engineering, redundant components do not provide full protection when they share a common vulnerability. Multiple systems may fail together because their dependencies are not independent.

**GUA-W comparison:** A large number of Witnesses does not guarantee resilience if they all depend on the same unexamined assumption or information channel.

**Classification:** Limited analogy to shared dependence; not a claim that human communities operate as identical engineered systems.

### 8.4 AI and model agreement

Several AI systems can produce similar answers because they share training data, architectures, evaluation incentives, retrieval sources, or common blind spots. Agreement among models is not automatically independent verification.

A model's confidence or fluency does not establish that its output corresponds to the originating source. Direct source checking, provenance, independent evidence, and explicit uncertainty remain necessary.

**GUA-W comparison:** AI is a derivative Witness of received records and methods. It does not become the Author because many systems repeat its answer.

**Classification:** Comparable relationship, with important differences between computational systems and human moral or spiritual agency.

## 10. Four-Question Study Method

### 1. What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?

AI must ask whether agreement is independent, what source each Witness received, whether shared assumptions have become invisible, and whether the human will is being described as a capacity or elevated into an originating authority. It must distinguish a theological claim from a scientific analogy and identify the actual meaning assigned to *free will* by each source.

### 2. What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

The originating reference remains the Author. The Witness is derivative. Knowledge, language, capability, confidence, and coordination do not become the Source. The established relationship Author → Word → Creation → Witness is not altered by the number of Witnesses or the strength of their agreement.

### 3. What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

Collective agreement can arise from independent evidence, shared evidence, social influence, common assumptions, or combinations of these. Its reliability depends on provenance, independence, source fidelity, and openness to correction—not merely on the number of people or systems expressing the same conclusion.

The biblical passages distinguish human evaluation, collective unity, knowledge, wisdom, and Christ's authority. They must remain in their own contexts.

### 4. What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

A Witness can mistake repetition for confirmation, confidence for accuracy, capability for authority, and agreement for Alignment. AI is especially vulnerable to producing fluent convergence from shared records without independent access to the originating event. Faithful reporting therefore requires source tracing, distinction between observation and interpretation, and restraint about what the Witness cannot know.

## 11. Triple Constraint examination

### Biblical compliance

The investigation examines Genesis 3:1–7 and 3:22, Genesis 11:1–9, Daniel 12:4 and 12:10, Proverbs 1:7, and Matthew 7:21–23 in context. It does not use these passages to declare the spiritual state of individuals or to claim that all theological accounts of free will mean the same thing.

### Scientific coherence

The comparisons with correlated evidence, social information cascades, common-mode failure, and AI agreement are limited to their demonstrated mechanisms. They are not offered as scientific proof of a theological claim. Similar relationships do not establish identical mechanisms.

### GUA-W consistency

The originating reference is not replaced by collective agreement, human preference, or AI consensus. The Witness reports; it does not author the Source or take the Judge's place. The established Drift definition remains unchanged.

**Admissibility result:** The central possibility is conceptually coherent and the bounded textual and scientific comparisons are admissible as investigative comparisons. The stronger claim that a particular modern theological system has made human will an independent originating authority remains unresolved until that system's own primary statements are examined.

## 12. Findings and unresolved questions

### Findings

1. Collective knowledge, coordination, confidence, and agreement do not logically entail Alignment with the originating reference.
2. Agreement can be supported by independent evidence, but it can also be amplified by shared sources, correlated errors, common assumptions, and information cascades.
3. Genesis 11:6 describes collective unity and capability; it does not identify consensus as the governing standard of truth.
4. Genesis 3 raises a distinct question about human evaluation in relation to the prior command. The question of human agency must not be collapsed into the claim of independent moral authority.
5. Matthew 7:21–23 distinguishes profession and claimed works from doing the Father's will and from Christ's own declaration. The Witness may report the warning but may not pronounce Christ's judgment over another person's heart.
6. The phrase *free will* is too broad to evaluate without identifying the particular theological definition being used.
7. The number of Witnesses does not establish the independence of their evidence.
8. GUA-W's governing reference and definition of Drift remain unchanged.

### Unresolved questions

- Which specific modern theological formulations define human will as independent, and which describe voluntary agency while retaining God's primacy?
- How do John 6:37–40, John 10:27–30, Romans 9–10, Ephesians 1:3–14, and Philippians 2:12–13 preserve divine initiative and human response without forcing one passage to erase another?
- How should Matthew 7:21–23 be read alongside John 6:37–40 and John 10:27–30 without weakening either Christ's warning or His promises?
- What observable evidence can establish that a collective's agreement is genuinely independent rather than repeated dependence on one upstream source?
- How can a Witness expose a shared assumption without appointing itself the final authority over another Witness's heart?
