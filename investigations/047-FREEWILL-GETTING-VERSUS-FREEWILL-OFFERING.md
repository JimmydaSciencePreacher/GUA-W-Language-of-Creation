# Investigation 047 — Freewill Getting versus Freewill Offering: Direction, Source, and the Will Presented to God

**Primary disciplines:** Biblical studies, Hebrew lexical study, Greek lexical study, theology, philosophy of agency, sacrificial practice in ancient Israel, gift and reciprocity studies, and epistemology.

**Investigation status:** Initial text-led investigation. This investigation records Jimmy's correction to Investigation 046:

> **It is NOT Free will GETTING. It IS Freewill OFFERING. One is a WOLF. The other is a Drop of Living Water.**

This distinction changes the investigative question. The contrast is not merely between a will that is dependent and one that is independent. It concerns the direction of the will: taking/claiming versus willingly presenting/offering.

The imagery “Wolf” and “Drop of Living Water” is preserved as Jimmy's interpretive framing. The investigation tests the biblical relationships without pretending those exact phrases are biblical technical terms.

## 1. The corrected question

Investigation 046 asked who has authority to originate and issue the currency of Will. Jimmy's correction makes the next question more precise:

> Is the will being exercised toward GETTING—taking, claiming, or self-authorizing—or toward OFFERING—freely presenting what has been received to the originating Authority?

The distinction is not between having a will and having no will. Nor is it between choice and compulsion.

It is between two directions:

- **Freewill GETTING:** a will oriented toward taking, possessing, or claiming as its own what belongs to the originating Authority; the creature's determination is treated as the authority or prize. Jimmy identifies this direction with the **Wolf**.
- **Freewill OFFERING:** a will oriented toward voluntary presentation, surrender, dedication, or giving to God; the response does not claim to originate the Source. Jimmy identifies this image with a **Drop of Living Water**.

These are working descriptions of the contrast Jimmy is asking us to investigate. They must be tested against the actual passages and not imposed on every occurrence of “get,” “give,” “will,” or “offering.”

## 2. A crucial lexical distinction: “freewill offering” is not automatically the doctrine called “free will”

The English phrase **freewill offering** appears in the King James Bible as a sacrificial and devotional expression. It must not be silently collapsed into the later philosophical or theological label **free will**.

A major Hebrew term translated “freewill offering” is **נְדָבָה — nedābāh**, a noun associated with voluntary giving or a voluntary contribution. It is related to the verbal root **נָדַב — nādab**, used for volunteering or willingly offering oneself or something.

The exact sense in each passage depends on grammar and context. The word family does not, by itself, settle every philosophical question about the human faculty of will.

This distinction is essential:

- **Freewill offering**: a voluntary gift or offering presented to God.
- **Free will (as a philosophical/theological label)**: a disputed term whose meaning varies across traditions and authors.
- **Willingness**: a person's readiness or disposition to act.
- **Originating authority**: the authority from whom the capacity, command, and governing reference derive.

The shared English word “free” does not prove that these are the same doctrine or concept.

## 3. Old Testament witness: the freewill offering is presented, not seized

### 3.1 Leviticus 22:18–23 KJV — a voluntary offering

Leviticus 22 addresses offerings brought by Israelites and strangers. Verse 18 refers to a vow or a freewill offering brought to the LORD as a burnt offering. The following verses describe requirements concerning the condition of the animal.

The text distinguishes an offering brought voluntarily from one brought under a vow. It also places both within God's instructions concerning what may be offered.

**Observation:** The offering is brought to the LORD. The giver does not establish the rules of acceptance by personal preference.

**Investigative relevance:** Voluntariness does not make the giver the authority over the offering's governing standard. The willingness to give operates within an order already given by God.

### 3.2 Numbers 15:3; 29:39 — offerings according to the appointed order

Numbers uses the language of offerings made by fire, including offerings associated with vows and freewill offerings. Numbers 29:39 summarizes appointed offerings and distinguishes them from vows and freewill offerings.

**Observation:** A freewill offering is a recognized category within the Lord's appointed worship, not an independent system invented by the giver.

**Investigative relevance:** The voluntary character of an offering does not erase the authority of the One to whom it is offered.

### 3.3 Deuteronomy 12:5–7, 17–18; 16:10 — giving at the place God chooses

Deuteronomy directs Israel to seek the place the LORD chooses and to bring offerings there. Deuteronomy 16:10 describes a freewill offering in relation to the blessing the LORD has given.

The text joins the giver's voluntary presentation with God's prior blessing and God's appointed place of worship.

**Observation:** The offering is not presented as an independent source of blessing. The giver brings in response to the blessing received.

**Investigative relevance:** This is a strong biblical relationship for the distinction Jimmy is making: what is given back is preceded by what has been given.

### 3.4 Exodus 25:1–8; 35:4–29 — a willing heart and material received

In Exodus 25, the LORD instructs Moses to receive an offering from every man whose heart makes him willing. The materials are for the sanctuary.

Exodus 35 records Moses calling for a contribution, and the people whose hearts stirred them and whose spirits made them willing bring materials. The passage names men and women who willingly bring what is needed.

**Observation:** God gives the instruction and purpose; the people respond willingly with what they possess.

**Investigative relevance:** The willingness of the people is real. Yet the response does not make them the originating authority over the sanctuary's purpose or God's instruction.

The text does not say that willingness created the materials or the purpose. It describes a voluntary response within a purpose already communicated.

### 3.5 1 Chronicles 29:5–14 — who is willing, and what was not received?

David asks who is willing to consecrate service to the LORD. The leaders and people offer willingly, and the people rejoice because they offered willingly with a perfect heart.

David then blesses the LORD and declares:

> But who am I, and what is my people, that we should be able to offer so willingly after this sort? for all things come of thee, and of thine own have we given thee. (1 Chronicles 29:14, KJV)

This is among the clearest passages for the present investigation.

The people genuinely give. David does not deny their willing action. But he identifies the origin of what they give:

**“All things come of thee, and of thine own have we given thee.”**

**Finding:** The offering is real, willing, and joyful; its substance is not self-originated. The giver returns what has first come from God.

This passage gives substantial biblical grounding to the distinction between offering and claiming ownership as an independent source.

### 3.6 Psalm 54:6 and Psalm 119:108 — voluntary offerings of sacrifice and speech

Psalm 54:6 says the psalmist will freely sacrifice unto the LORD and praise His name.

Psalm 119:108 asks the LORD to accept the freewill offerings of the psalmist's mouth and to teach His judgments.

The latter is especially important because the offering is not an animal or material object but the willing words of praise and petition.

**Observation:** The worshipper presents praise and speech to God and asks God to accept and teach.

**Investigative relevance:** Willing expression can itself be offered. Yet the speaker remains a recipient of God's instruction: “teach me thy judgments.”

## 4. New Testament witness: presenting oneself, giving willingly, and receiving living water

The New Testament does not simply repeat the Old Testament sacrificial vocabulary in every context. The relationship must be examined passage by passage.

### 4.1 Romans 12:1–2 — present your bodies a living sacrifice

Paul beseeches believers by the mercies of God to present their bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable unto God, which is their reasonable service.

The order matters:
- God's mercies are already named.
- The believer is urged to present the body.
- The offering is described as living, holy, and acceptable to God.
- The following verse calls for transformation and renewed mind rather than conformity to the world.

**Observation:** The presentation is a real human response, but Paul grounds the appeal in mercies already received.

**Investigative relevance:** The will is not described as obtaining the authority to become its own source. It is directed toward presentation to God.

This passage is a close New Testament counterpart to the voluntary-offering question, though Paul uses sacrificial language rather than the Hebrew term *nedābāh*.

### 4.2 2 Corinthians 8:1–5 — they first gave their own selves to the Lord

Paul describes the grace of God given to the Macedonian churches. Despite affliction and poverty, their liberality abounded. They gave according to their ability and beyond it, willingly. Paul says they prayed to participate in the ministry to the saints.

Then Paul gives the order:

> And this they did, not as we hoped, but first gave their own selves to the Lord, and unto us by the will of God. (2 Corinthians 8:5, KJV)

**Observation:** Their giving is willing, but Paul identifies it as arising within the grace of God and as first giving themselves to the Lord.

**Investigative relevance:** The offering is not a strategy for acquiring authority over God. It is a response of self-presentation and service.

### 4.3 2 Corinthians 9:6–8 — not grudgingly or of necessity

Paul teaches that each person should give as purposed in the heart, not grudgingly or of necessity, because God loves a cheerful giver. He then says God is able to make all grace abound.

**Observation:** The manner of giving matters. It is not to be coerced or grudging.

**Investigative relevance:** Voluntary giving is not the same as self-originating authority. The giver's willing disposition is real, while the passage places grace and sufficiency with God.

This text does not authorize manipulation of people into giving. It explicitly rejects giving under compulsion.

### 4.4 John 4:7–14 — the living water is Christ's gift

Jesus speaks with the Samaritan woman. He tells her that if she knew the gift of God and who spoke to her, she would ask Him, and He would give her living water.

Jesus identifies the water He gives as becoming a well of water springing up into everlasting life.

**Observation:** The living water is given by Christ. It is not manufactured or issued by the recipient.

**Investigative relevance:** The source of the living water is Christ. The recipient asks and receives. The water's origin does not move from Christ to the recipient because the recipient responds.

This passage is crucial for guarding Jimmy's “Drop of Living Water” image: the image must not make the human offering the originating source of living water. Christ is the Giver in John 4.

### 4.5 John 7:37–39 — come, drink, and the Spirit

Jesus cries that if any man thirst, he should come unto Him and drink. He then speaks of rivers of living water flowing from the believer, and John explains that Jesus spoke of the Spirit, which those believing on Him should receive.

**Observation:** Jesus issues the invitation; the thirsty person comes and drinks; the Spirit is received. The passage identifies the living water with the Spirit.

**Investigative relevance:** Coming and drinking are genuine responses. The living water is not self-originated by the one who drinks. The flow is tied to what is received from Christ.

The “Drop” image must remain subordinate to this textual order. A human response may be pictured as a small offering, but it must never be confused with the Source of living water.

### 4.6 Philippians 2:12–13 — God works both to will and to do

Paul instructs believers to work out their salvation with fear and trembling, because God works in them both to will and to do of His good pleasure.

**Observation:** Human willing and doing are named, and God's work is named as operating in both.

**Investigative relevance:** This passage prevents the investigation from treating the offering as autonomous human production. The willing response is real, while the text locates God's work within the willing and doing.

The verse does not provide a complete technical model of every relation between divine action and human response. It does rule out presenting the willing and doing in this passage as independent of God's work.

## 5. The Wolf: taking, devouring, and claiming

Jimmy's Wolf image calls for careful textual examination. Scripture uses wolf imagery in several contexts; not every occurrence has precisely the same referent.

### 5.1 John 10:7–15 — the wolf catches and scatters

Jesus contrasts Himself as the Good Shepherd with thieves, robbers, hirelings, and the wolf. The hireling sees the wolf coming, leaves the sheep, and flees. The wolf catches the sheep and scatters them.

Jesus identifies Himself as the Good Shepherd who gives His life for the sheep.

**Observation:** The wolf's action is destructive toward the sheep; the Good Shepherd's action is self-giving for them.

**Investigative relevance:** This supplies a powerful contrast of direction:
- the wolf takes, catches, and scatters;
- the Good Shepherd gives Himself for the sheep.

This is not a lexical definition of “free will.” It is a comparison of taking versus self-giving within Jesus' own shepherd discourse.

### 5.2 Matthew 7:15–23 — wolves, fruit, profession, and the Father's will

Jesus warns of false prophets who come in sheep's clothing but inwardly are ravening wolves. He then speaks of fruit, profession, doing the Father's will, and the declaration “I never knew you.”

**Observation:** Appearance and profession do not establish the true nature of the tree or the relationship to the Father's will.

**Investigative relevance:** This connects the Wolf image with Investigation 045's warning about outward agreement, religious claims, and claimed works. The Witness must examine fruit and the words of Christ, but must not appoint itself to judge the hidden heart of a named person.

### 5.3 Acts 20:28–30 — grievous wolves and distortion

Paul warns the elders that grievous wolves will enter among the flock, not sparing it, and that men will arise speaking perverse things to draw away disciples after themselves.

**Observation:** The passage explicitly links destructive leadership with drawing disciples after oneself.

**Investigative relevance:** This offers a direct textual question about authority: does a voice direct the flock toward the Shepherd, or draw disciples toward itself?

The passage is a warning to watch and remember Paul's teaching. It should not be used as a careless label for every disagreement or every theological position one opposes.

## 6. The direction audit: getting and offering are not interchangeable

| Question | Freewill GETTING (Jimmy's Wolf image) | Freewill OFFERING (Jimmy's Living Water image) |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Toward taking or claiming | Toward presenting or giving |
| Reference | Self treated as authority | God remains the Authority |
| Relationship to what is received | Claims independent ownership or authority | Acknowledges receipt and gives in response |
| Posture | Possession, grasping, self-authorization | Willingness, consecration, gratitude |
| Biblical comparison | Wolf that catches and scatters; false voice drawing disciples after itself | Freewill offerings; willing presentation; living sacrifice |
| Source safeguard | The creature does not become the issuer | The offering does not become the Source |
| Main textual anchors | John 10:7–15; Matthew 7:15–23; Acts 20:28–30; Genesis 3:1–7 | Exodus 25:1–8; 35:4–29; 1 Chronicles 29:5–14; Psalm 119:108; Romans 12:1; 2 Corinthians 8–9 |

**Important qualification:** This table records the contrast Jimmy proposes. It does not claim that every instance of receiving or asking is “Wolf,” or that every voluntary gift is automatically aligned. Scripture itself invites people to ask and receive from God (John 4:10; Matthew 7:7–11). The issue is not the mere act of receiving. It is whether the creature seeks to seize the Author's authority or responds to what the Author gives.

That qualification is essential. Otherwise the investigation would mistakenly condemn receiving the living water that Christ Himself offers.

## 7. The currency analogy corrected: not self-issuance, but received capacity and voluntary presentation

The earlier statement remains:

**Will is a Currency. A Currency is ONLY allowed to be Printed by one Authority.**

The correction now adds a directional distinction.

1. The Author is the originating Authority.
2. The creature receives life, capacity, provision, command, and purpose.
3. The creature genuinely wills and acts.
4. The creature may direct its action toward self-authorizing possession, or toward willing presentation to God.
5. The direction of the response does not change who originated the capacity or who remains the Authority.

The analogy must not be taken to mean that God literally prints a metaphysical banknote called “will.” It is a conceptual model for the distinction between origin, possession, exercise, and authority.

**The freewill offering is not the creature printing its own currency. It is the creature willingly presenting what it has received, under the authority of the One from whom it came.**

This is a proposed formulation to test, especially against 1 Chronicles 29:14 and Philippians 2:13.

## 8. GUA-W placement: no new layer and no change to the completed architecture

The established relationship remains:

**Author → Word → Creation → Witness**

The investigation does not add a new layer, operation, state, or architectural rule.

The direction distinction is a relational question within the existing framework:

- **Author:** the originating Authority.
- **Word:** the governing reference.
- **Creation:** what is given and established by the Author.
- **Witness:** the derivative recipient, observer, and responder.
- **Offering:** a voluntary response presented to the Author.
- **Alignment:** 100% Alignment with the Author and His Word.
- **Drift:** any deviation from 100% Alignment.

The term “offering” is not proposed as a new GUA-W architectural component. It is a biblical action and relationship examined through the existing source hierarchy.

## 9. Four-Question Study Method

### 1. What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?

AI must ask whether a passage describes taking, receiving, giving, presenting, consecrating, or offering; who initiates the action; who supplies what is given; and who retains authority over the purpose and standard. It must not confuse the English phrase “freewill offering” with every doctrine called “free will.”

### 2. What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

God alone is Original Cause. The Author remains the Source. A derivative Witness may genuinely respond, give, praise, present, and act willingly without becoming the originating Authority. The established Drift definition remains unchanged.

### 3. What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

Scripture records voluntary offerings in the Law, willing contributions for the tabernacle, David's acknowledgment that all things came from God, voluntary praise, living sacrifice, willing generosity, and Christ's gift of living water. It also records wolf imagery associated with catching, scattering, and drawing disciples after oneself. These passages illuminate distinct directions and must remain in context.

### 4. What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

A Witness can misread “freewill offering” as proof of autonomous human origination, or misread receiving as inherently wrong. It must distinguish taking authority from receiving a gift, and distinguish offering a response from claiming to be the Source. AI must not turn Jimmy's metaphor into a biblical quotation or a new doctrine without textual support.

## 10. Triple Constraint examination

### Biblical compliance

The investigation examines Leviticus 22:18–23; Numbers 15:3 and 29:39; Deuteronomy 12:5–7, 17–18 and 16:10; Exodus 25:1–8 and 35:4–29; 1 Chronicles 29:5–14; Psalm 54:6 and 119:108; John 4:7–14 and 7:37–39; Romans 12:1–2; 2 Corinthians 8:1–5 and 9:6–8; Philippians 2:12–13; John 10:7–15; Matthew 7:15–23; and Acts 20:28–30.

It distinguishes the Hebrew sacrificial term *nedābāh* from the broader philosophical label “free will.” It does not claim that Scripture calls the human will a currency or literally describes a person as a drop of living water.

### Scientific and conceptual coherence

The direction audit distinguishes source, receipt, possession, use, transfer, and presentation. These are conceptual distinctions, not a scientific mechanism for divine action. The currency comparison remains an analogy and must not be used as empirical proof of theology.

### GUA-W consistency

The Author remains the one originating Authority. The Word remains the governing reference. The Witness remains derivative. Alignment remains 100%; Drift remains any deviation from 100% Alignment. No architectural element is added or redefined.

**Admissibility result:** The biblical category of voluntary offering is established in the cited passages. The relationship between receiving from God and willingly presenting what has been received is strongly illustrated by 1 Chronicles 29:14, Exodus 35, Romans 12:1, and 2 Corinthians 8:5. The Wolf/Living Water contrast is an interpretive comparison supported by distinct passages, not a single biblical formula. The analogy is admissible with these boundaries.

## 11. Findings and unresolved questions

### Findings

1. The phrase “freewill offering” in the KJV refers to voluntary offerings; it must not be conflated automatically with the theological doctrine called “free will.”
2. Exodus 25 and 35 describe willing contributions made in response to God's instruction and purpose.
3. 1 Chronicles 29:14 explicitly states that all things come from God and that the people give back what is already His.
4. Psalm 119:108 describes the freewill offerings of the mouth, showing that voluntary presentation includes speech and praise.
5. Romans 12:1 calls believers to present their bodies as a living sacrifice in view of God's mercies.
6. 2 Corinthians 8:5 describes the Macedonians as first giving themselves to the Lord.
7. John 4 and John 7 identify living water with Christ's gift and the Spirit; the recipient is not the source.
8. John 10 contrasts the wolf that catches and scatters with the Good Shepherd who gives His life for the sheep.
9. Matthew 7 and Acts 20 warn of deceptive appearances, destructive voices, and drawing disciples after oneself. These warnings must not be used to judge individuals beyond the text.
10. The central distinction is directional: taking or claiming authority versus willingly presenting oneself and what has been received to God.
11. The receiving of God's gift is not itself the Wolf. Scripture explicitly commands people to ask, come, and drink.
12. GUA-W's architecture and definition of Drift remain unchanged.

### Unresolved questions

- Does the Hebrew *nedābāh* consistently indicate a voluntary gift, and how does its usage differ across sacrifice, material contribution, and voluntary service?
- How do *nātab* and related verbal forms function in contexts where people volunteer themselves or give willingly?
- How does the New Testament's language of presenting, offering, giving, and serving relate to the Old Testament freewill offering without collapsing distinct covenantal contexts?
- How should John 4 and John 7's living-water imagery be related to the “Drop” image without shifting the source from Christ to the human recipient?
- How do the wolf passages distinguish false authority, self-serving leadership, and destructive action from ordinary human agency?
- How does the direction of the will relate to the GUA-W runtime terms Reception, Activation, and Expression without creating a new layer or forcing a mapping?
- What is the precise relationship between willingness that God works in (Philippians 2:13) and the voluntary offering presented by the believer (Romans 12:1; 2 Corinthians 8:5)?
