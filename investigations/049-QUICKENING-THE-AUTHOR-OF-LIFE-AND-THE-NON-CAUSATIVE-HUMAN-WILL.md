# Investigation 049 — Quickening: The Author of Life and the Non-Causative Human Will

**Primary disciplines:** Biblical studies, New Testament Greek, Hebrew and Greek lexical study, theology, doctrine of salvation, philosophy of causation and agency, epistemology, and AI evaluation.

**Investigation status:** Initial text-led investigation. This investigation follows Jimmy's direct correction:

> **Man will is NOT the Cause of quickening. There is ONE recorded NAME who Quickeneth.**

The central claim to test is that human willing is not the originating cause of spiritual quickening. The human response must not be placed before, above, or in place of the life-giving action Scripture attributes to God.

The investigation will identify exactly how the KJV text attributes quickening to the Father, the Son, and the Spirit, and how those passages relate to the human will. It will not assume that the English word “quickeneth” occurs in only one verse or that all passages use the same grammatical subject. It will preserve the biblical wording and examine the theological unity and distinctions rather than flatten them.

## 1. Correcting the causal order

The previous investigations examined:
- the human will as a derivative capacity, not an independent originating authority;
- freewill offering as a voluntary presentation, not self-originating production;
- the Wolf in the Church as a possible teaching that misattributes the source of a response or offering.

Jimmy now identifies a more foundational issue:

**The will of a spiritually dead person is not the cause of that person's quickening.**

This is a question of causation and order. It is not a denial that Scripture describes people as willing, believing, coming, asking, repenting, confessing, offering, and acting. It asks whether any such human action is identified as the originating cause of the life by which the person is made alive.

The investigation must distinguish:

1. **Quickening:** God's life-giving action.
2. **Willing:** a real human disposition or act.
3. **Coming and believing:** responses described in Scripture.
4. **Offering:** a voluntary presentation to God.
5. **Doing:** action that follows and expresses the life and purpose given.

These are not interchangeable terms. A response must not be silently converted into the cause of the life that makes the response possible.

## 2. John 5:19–29 — the Father gives life; the Son quickens

John 5 is a primary passage because it directly names the Father and the Son in relation to life.

John 5:21 KJV:

> For as the Father raiseth up the dead, and quickeneth them; even so the Son quickeneth whom he will.

The surrounding context is essential:
- The Son does what He sees the Father do (5:19).
- The Father raises the dead and quickens them (5:21).
- The Father has committed judgment to the Son (5:22).
- The Son is to be honoured even as the Father is honoured (5:23).
- The one who hears Christ's word and believes the Father has everlasting life and is passed from death unto life (5:24).
- The hour is coming, and now is, when the dead shall hear the voice of the Son of God, and they that hear shall live (5:25).
- As the Father hath life in Himself, so hath He given to the Son to have life in Himself (5:26).

### Findings from the passage

The text explicitly attributes quickening to the Father and to the Son. It does not attribute the originating life-giving act to the human will.

The Son's quickening is not presented as an independent creaturely power. The surrounding passage describes the relation of the Son to the Father, while also insisting that the Son gives life and is to be honoured.

**Critical question:** When the Son says that the dead hear His voice and live, does the passage make the dead person's prior will the cause of life? The text identifies the Son's voice and life-giving authority. It must not be rewritten to make the human will the quickener.

The phrase “whom he will” in verse 21 refers to the Son's will, not the human will. The grammatical subject must be preserved.

## 3. John 6:35–65 — coming, believing, drawing, granting, and quickening

John 6 must be read as a connected argument, not as isolated proof-texts.

### 3.1 John 6:37–40 — given by the Father and received by the Son

Jesus says that all the Father gives Him shall come to Him, and that He will in no wise cast out the one who comes. He states that He came down from heaven not to do His own will, but the will of the Father who sent Him. The Father's will is that the Son lose nothing of all that the Father has given Him, but raise it up at the last day.

**Observation:** The passage identifies the Father's giving, the Son's receiving, the person's coming, and the Son's preserving and raising.

The coming is real. The Father's giving is also real and prior in the sentence's stated order. The Son's promise is not made dependent on an independent human originating authority.

### 3.2 John 6:44 — no man can come unless drawn

> No man can come to me, except the Father which hath sent me draw him: and I will raise him up at the last day.

The text states a limitation: no man can come unless the Father draws him. It also states the Son's promise to raise him at the last day.

This passage directly challenges any account that makes man's unaided will the originating cause of coming to Christ.

It does not make the human coming unreal. It locates the enabling initiative in the Father's drawing.

### 3.3 John 6:63 — the Spirit quickeneth

> It is the spirit that quickeneth; the flesh profiteth nothing: the words that I speak unto you, they are spirit, and they are life.

The grammatical subject is explicit: **the Spirit quickeneth**. The contrast is with the flesh, which profits nothing in the matter under discussion.

The verse must not be diluted into a statement that human determination supplies the life-giving cause. Jesus identifies the Spirit as the One who quickens and His words as spirit and life.

### 3.4 John 6:64–65 — belief and granting

Jesus knows from the beginning who believed not and who should betray Him. He then says:

> Therefore said I unto you, that no man can come unto me, except it were given unto him of my Father. (John 6:65, KJV)

This repeats the necessity of the Father's grant. The passage does not say that man's will first creates the ability to come and then causes the Father to give.

**Finding:** John 6 describes coming and believing, but it also explicitly identifies the Father's drawing and granting and the Spirit's quickening. A reading that makes autonomous human willing the originating cause must account for all these statements rather than omit them.

## 4. Romans 4:16–25 — God quickeneth the dead

Romans 4 discusses Abraham, promise, faith, and the God in whom Abraham believed.

Romans 4:17 KJV describes God as:

> ...even God, who quickeneth the dead, and calleth those things which be not as though they were.

The context describes Abraham's trust in God's promise despite the condition of his own body and Sarah's womb. Abraham's faith is real, but the passage directs attention to the God who gives life and fulfils His promise.

**Observation:** God is named as the One who quickens the dead.

**Investigative relevance:** Faith does not become the life-giving Author. Abraham believes the God who quickens. The object and source of faith must not be replaced by faith itself as an independent cause.

The passage concerns God's promise to Abraham and the righteousness of faith. Its application to spiritual quickening must be connected with other passages rather than treated as though Romans 4:17 alone were a complete systematic account of regeneration.

## 5. Romans 8:1–17 — the Spirit of life and the quickening of mortal bodies

Romans 8 contrasts life in the Spirit with life according to the flesh.

Romans 8:11 KJV:

> But if the Spirit of him that raised up Jesus from the dead dwell in you, he that raised up Christ from the dead shall also quicken your mortal bodies by his Spirit that dwelleth in you.

The verse names:
- the Spirit of Him who raised Jesus;
- God as the One who raised Christ;
- the promise to quicken mortal bodies;
- the Spirit dwelling in believers.

This verse explicitly concerns the future quickening of mortal bodies in the context of resurrection hope. It should not be collapsed without qualification into every aspect of initial spiritual regeneration. Yet it clearly preserves the source of life-giving power with God through His Spirit.

Romans 8:13 also says that if by the Spirit believers mortify the deeds of the body, they shall live. The human action is described, but it is “by the Spirit.” The text does not assign the Spirit's life-giving role to autonomous human will.

## 6. Ephesians 2:1–10 — dead in sins, made alive by God

Ephesians 2 is essential to the question of human will and quickening.

Paul describes the readers as dead in trespasses and sins (2:1). He describes their former walk and the influence of the world, the prince of the power of the air, and the desires of the flesh (2:2–3). Then comes the decisive transition:

> But God, who is rich in mercy, for his great love wherewith he loved us,
>
> Even when we were dead in sins, hath quickened us together with Christ, (by grace ye are saved;) (Ephesians 2:4–5, KJV)

The grammatical and theological movement is unmistakable:
- The people are described as dead in sins.
- God is rich in mercy.
- God acts because of His great love.
- God quickens them together with Christ.
- Salvation is by grace.

Verses 8–10 continue: salvation is by grace through faith, not of yourselves, not of works, lest any man should boast; believers are God's workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works.

### Investigative finding

The text does not say that people made themselves alive by willing to be alive. It says God quickened them when they were dead in sins.

The works follow the identity and workmanship described in the passage. They are not the originating cause of the new creation.

The relation between faith and the phrase “that not of yourselves” must be studied grammatically and contextually. The Greek demonstrative *touto* (“this”) is neuter, while *pistis* (“faith”) is feminine; this has led interpreters to discuss whether “this” refers to the whole salvation-by-grace-through-faith event or specifically to faith. The safest conclusion for this investigation is that the entire saving event is described as God's gift and not a basis for boasting. The grammatical observation alone does not settle every theological debate.

## 7. Titus 3:3–7 — not by works of righteousness which we have done

Paul describes the former condition of people as foolish, disobedient, deceived, and serving various desires. Then:

> But after that the kindness and love of God our Saviour toward man appeared,
>
> Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost; (Titus 3:4–5, KJV)

The passage attributes salvation to God's kindness, love, mercy, regeneration, and the renewing of the Holy Ghost.

It explicitly denies that salvation is by works of righteousness which we have done.

**Investigative relevance:** A human work cannot be promoted into the originating cause of regeneration when the passage identifies God's mercy and the Holy Ghost's renewing.

The passage does not deny that saved people subsequently do good works. Titus 3:8 urges believers to maintain good works. Again, the order and source matter.

## 8. 1 Timothy 6:13 — God, who quickeneth all things

Paul charges Timothy before God, who quickeneth all things, and before Jesus Christ, who witnessed a good confession before Pontius Pilate.

This is one of the clearest KJV formulations for Jimmy's stated point:

**“God, who quickeneth all things.”**

The verse names God in relation to quickening. It also names Jesus Christ in the same solemn charge. The passage must be read as written and not used to erase the New Testament's other explicit statements that the Son quickens (John 5:21) and the Spirit quickens (John 6:63).

### Important precision about “one recorded NAME”

Jimmy's phrase “ONE recorded NAME who Quickeneth” raises a necessary textual question: Is the intended referent the name **God**, the name **Jesus Christ**, or the one divine Source identified through the Father, Son, and Spirit across the passages?

The KJV uses “God” in 1 Timothy 6:13 and Romans 4:17; it identifies the Father and the Son in John 5:21; and it identifies the Spirit in John 6:63. These are not interchangeable grammatical subjects. Nor should the texts be forced into a conclusion that contradicts any of them.

This investigation therefore preserves the exact language and leaves the precise intended force of “one recorded NAME” as a question for Jimmy to clarify if he means a particular revealed name. What is already clear from the passages is that quickening is not attributed to autonomous human will.

## 9. Philippians 2:12–13 — God works in the willing and doing

This passage was central to the prior investigation and must now be read with the quickening distinction in place.

> Wherefore, my beloved, as ye have always obeyed, not as in my presence only, but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling.
>
> For it is God which worketh in you both to will and to do of his good pleasure.

Paul commands believers to work out their salvation. He then gives the reason: God works in them both to will and to do.

### What the passage establishes

- Human obedience and action are real.
- The command is addressed to believers.
- God's work is identified as operating in both willing and doing.
- The human will is not presented as an independent originating source detached from God.

### What the passage does not by itself establish

The passage does not use the verb “quicken” in these verses. It should not be cited as though it were itself the direct definition of quickening. Rather, it informs the relation between God's work and the believer's willing and doing after the saving context has been established.

**Correction to the previous investigation:** Philippians 2:13 must not be used to blur the distinction between God's life-giving act and the believer's response. It says God works to will and to do; Ephesians 2:5 directly says God quickened those dead in sins. Keep both statements in their proper place.

## 12. The Wolf's possible reversal of the order

The prior investigation identified the possibility that a Witness can be taught to believe he is giving God something that originated independently in himself.

This investigation identifies a deeper version of that possible reversal:

1. Scripture describes the person as dead in sins.
2. Scripture attributes quickening to God.
3. Scripture describes the Father drawing and granting.
4. Scripture describes the Spirit quickening.
5. Scripture describes the person as coming, believing, willing, offering, and doing.
6. A theological teaching may reverse the order and present the human will as the originating cause of the life-giving event.
7. The person may then be taught to regard the human decision as the decisive contribution that makes the difference, rather than as a response whose possibility and life depend upon God's prior action.

This is the specific teaching claim to investigate, not an accusation to apply indiscriminately to every Christian tradition or individual.

**The test:** Does the teaching say that the human will responds to God's quickening and grace, or does it make the human will the cause that quickens itself or independently causes spiritual life?

The actual words of the theologian or confession must be examined before assigning either description.

## 13. The Four-Question Study Method

### 1. What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?

AI must ask: Who is the grammatical subject of “quickeneth”? Who acts? Who is dead? What is the stated cause? What is the stated response? What is the sequence in the passage? Does the text attribute the life-giving act to God, the Father, the Son, or the Spirit? Does a proposed interpretation move the human will into a role the text assigns elsewhere?

AI must not confuse the Son's “whom he will” in John 5:21 with human will.

### 2. What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

The Author is the Source of life. The Witness cannot originate the life that makes the Witness alive. Human willing and doing may be genuine without becoming the cause of quickening. A derivative response must not be promoted into Original Cause.

### 3. What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

The KJV repeatedly attributes quickening to God and explicitly to the Father, Son, and Spirit in different passages. Ephesians 2 describes God quickening people who were dead in sins. John 6 identifies the Spirit as the One who quickens and the Father as the One who draws and grants. Philippians 2 identifies God as working in believers both to will and to do.

### 4. What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

A Witness may mistake the response for the cause, the coming for the drawing, the offering for the gift's origin, or the decision for the quickening. AI can make the same mistake when it reads a verse about human response and ignores the passage that identifies God's prior action. The Witness must preserve the order and the grammatical subject.
