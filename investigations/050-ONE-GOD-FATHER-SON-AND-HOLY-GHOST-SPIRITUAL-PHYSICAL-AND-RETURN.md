# Investigation 050 — One God: Father, Son, and Holy Ghost; Spiritual, Physical, and Return

**Primary disciplines:** Biblical studies, Johannine literature, New Testament Greek, theology, Christology, pneumatology, doctrine of the Trinity, incarnation and resurrection studies, philosophy of causation, and epistemology.

**Investigation status:** Initial text-led investigation. This investigation follows Jimmy's clarification:

> **There is ONE GOD who is “expressed” in three forms to perform three “Duties.” This IS a Spiritual to Physical and back again situation.**

This is recorded as Jimmy's proposed explanatory model and investigated against Scripture. It is not silently converted into a settled doctrinal formula. The biblical texts must determine what can be said about the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost, and how the spiritual and physical dimensions relate.

The central question is:

> How does the one God reveal and act as Father, Son, and Holy Ghost across the movement from the Word, to flesh, to death and resurrection, to the Spirit's life-giving work—without dividing God into three gods, collapsing the Father, Son, and Spirit into one undifferentiated role, or assigning quickening to the human will?

## 1. Clarifying the terms before mapping them

Jimmy's language is:
- **One God**
- **Three expressions/forms**
- **Three duties**
- **Spiritual → Physical → Spiritual**

These terms are a working proposal. They require careful definition.

In historic Nicene Christian theology, the standard formulation is **one God in three Persons: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit**. It does not ordinarily describe the three as three temporary forms or masks. The Father is not the Son; the Son is not the Spirit; and yet the three are not three gods. This traditional formulation is not being imposed as the answer to Jimmy's model; it is a necessary doctrinal distinction to keep visible while comparing claims.

Scripture itself uses relational language that must not be flattened:
- The Word is with God and is God (John 1:1).
- The Word becomes flesh (John 1:14).
- The Father sends the Son (John 3:16–17; Galatians 4:4).
- The Son speaks of the Father who sent Him (John 5:19–23; 6:38).
- The Father sends the Comforter in the Son's name (John 14:26).
- The Son says He will send the Comforter from the Father (John 15:26).
- The Spirit proceeds from the Father and testifies of the Son (John 15:26; 16:13–15).

The investigation therefore distinguishes **unity of divine identity/source** from **distinction of personal relation and mission**. “Three duties” may be a useful initial map of certain actions, but the passages must be checked before assigning each Person an exclusive duty. John 5:21, for example, says both the Father and the Son quicken. John 6:63 says the Spirit quickens. The texts do not support a simplistic division in which only one Person ever participates in life-giving action.

## 2. John 1:1–18 — the Word was God and was made flesh

John 1:1 KJV:

> In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.

John 1:14 KJV:

> And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.

The opening of John presents both unity and distinction:
- The Word was **with God**.
- The Word **was God**.
- The Word was made flesh.
- The glory beheld is described in relation to the only begotten of the Father.

### Findings

The incarnation is not the creation of a second, independent originating God. Nor does the text describe the Word as merely an appearance with no real humanity. The Word became flesh and dwelt among humanity.

The passage gives a strong textual basis for examining a movement from divine Word to embodied, physical human life. It does not mean the divine nature was converted into created matter or ceased to be divine. “Was made flesh” must be read in the full Johannine context, including the Word's pre-existence and identity.

**GUA-W connection:** The Author and Word relationship is central, but the Witness must not confuse the Word with a derivative human account of the Word. The incarnation is God's act, not a human will's production.

## 3. John 5:19–29 — the Father and the Son give life

John 5:21 KJV:

> For as the Father raiseth up the dead, and quickeneth them; even so the Son quickeneth whom he will.

John 5:26 KJV:

> For as the Father hath life in himself; so hath he given to the Son to have life in himself;

The passage identifies the Father and the Son distinctly while attributing life-giving authority to both. The Son's action is described in relation to the Father and the Father's giving.

John 5:25 says the dead shall hear the voice of the Son of God, and they that hear shall live.

### Findings

- The Father quickens.
- The Son quickens.
- The Son's life-giving authority is discussed in relation to the Father.
- The dead hear the Son's voice and live.
- The human will is not named as the quickening cause.

This passage prevents an exclusive “one Person does all quickening while the others have no part” allocation. It also prevents the opposite error of erasing the distinction between Father and Son.

## 4. John 6:35–65 — the Father's drawing, the Son's words, and the Spirit's quickening

John 6 presents several related actions:
- The Father gives people to the Son (6:37).
- The Son receives those who come and promises not to cast them out (6:37).
- The Father draws (6:44).
- The Son raises the drawn person at the last day (6:44).
- The Spirit quickens (6:63).
- The Father's grant is necessary for coming (6:65).

John 6:63 KJV:

> It is the spirit that quickeneth; the flesh profiteth nothing: the words that I speak unto you, they are spirit, and they are life.

### Findings

The passage does not permit human will to become the originating cause of quickening. It also does not permit the investigator to separate the Spirit's quickening from the Son's life-giving words and the Father's drawing and granting as though Scripture presented unrelated mechanisms.

The statement “the flesh profiteth nothing” is made in this discourse about the Spirit's life-giving work. It must not be expanded into a claim that the physical creation or Christ's embodied humanity is evil or unreal. John 1:14 and the resurrection texts rule out such a reading.

## 5. John 14:1–26; 15:26; 16:7–15 — the Father, Son, and Comforter

These chapters are indispensable because Jesus speaks directly about His relation to the Father and the coming of the Comforter.

- John 14:6: Jesus identifies Himself as the way, the truth, and the life.
- John 14:9–11: Jesus speaks of the Father dwelling in Him and of the works.
- John 14:16–17: Jesus says He will pray the Father, who will give another Comforter.
- John 14:26: the Father sends the Holy Ghost in Jesus' name.
- John 15:26: Jesus says He will send the Comforter from the Father, the Spirit of truth, who proceeds from the Father and testifies of the Son.
- John 16:13–15: the Spirit of truth guides, speaks what He hears, glorifies the Son, and receives of what is Christ's.

### Findings

The text presents unity of divine work and distinction of relation:
- The Father sends.
- The Son asks and sends.
- The Spirit is sent, comes, teaches, guides, and testifies.
- The Spirit glorifies the Son rather than drawing attention to an independent rival source.

This is not a simple chart of three exclusive jobs. It is a set of relations and missions within the one divine work.

**Investigative caution:** “Three duties” may summarize selected functions, but the biblical passages show overlapping action and mutual relation. A duty chart must remain subordinate to the text.

## 6. The incarnation: spiritual to physical

John 1:14 is the direct anchor. Other passages clarify the event:
- Galatians 4:4: God sent forth His Son, made of a woman, made under the law.
- Philippians 2:5–8: Christ Jesus, being in the form of God, took the form of a servant and was made in the likeness of men.
- Hebrews 2:14–17: the children are partakers of flesh and blood, and Christ likewise took part of the same.
- 1 Timothy 3:16: God was manifest in the flesh (KJV wording).

The incarnation is a real entry into human physical existence. It is not merely a human being imagining or representing God. Nor does the incarnation mean that God's divine being was reduced to, or exhausted by, the physical body.

### GUA-W relevance

The Word's manifestation in flesh is an Author-originated act. It is not the human Witness generating the Source. The physical manifestation reveals the Word; it does not replace the Word's origin.

The movement “Spiritual → Physical” can therefore be used as a descriptive map of the incarnation, provided it is not taken to mean that divine Spirit became a different substance or ceased to be God.

## 7. Death and resurrection: the physical is not discarded

The phrase “and back again” requires careful examination. The New Testament does not describe resurrection as the Son abandoning physical existence and returning to a purely disembodied state.

### 7.1 John 20:19–29 — the risen Christ

The risen Jesus shows the disciples His hands and side. Thomas is invited to see and touch. The resurrection is presented as bodily and continuous with the crucified Jesus.

### 7.2 Luke 24:36–43 — flesh and bones

Jesus tells the disciples to behold His hands and feet and says:

> ...for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have. (Luke 24:39, KJV)

He then eats before them. The text emphasizes the reality of His risen body.

### 7.3 1 Corinthians 15:42–49 — raised a spiritual body

Paul contrasts corruption and incorruption, dishonour and glory, weakness and power, natural body and spiritual body. “Spiritual body” must not be misread as “non-body.” Paul explicitly calls it a body.

The resurrection is not the rejection of physical embodiment. It is transformation from corruption to incorruption, from mortality to immortality.

### Finding

The biblical movement is not simply:

**Spirit → matter → escape from matter.**

A more faithful provisional description is:

**The Word made flesh → bodily death → bodily resurrection and glorification → the Spirit's life-giving work and the promised resurrection of believers.**

This preserves the physical reality of incarnation and resurrection while recognizing the Spirit's life-giving role.

## 8. Romans 8:9–23 — the Spirit indwells and will quicken mortal bodies

Romans 8 speaks of the Spirit of God dwelling in believers and says that the One who raised Christ from the dead will also quicken their mortal bodies by His Spirit dwelling in them (8:11).

The passage moves from:
- Christ's resurrection;
- the Spirit dwelling in believers;
- the promise concerning mortal bodies;
- adoption and the redemption of the body;
- creation's deliverance from corruption.

This is particularly relevant to Jimmy's “Spiritual to Physical and back again” description. The Spirit's work is not confined to an inward idea or a temporary religious feeling. The passage connects the indwelling Spirit with the future redemption and quickening of the body.

**Finding:** The biblical trajectory includes bodily redemption. It does not describe the physical as an irrelevant shell to be discarded.

## 9. Three expressions and three duties: a testable table, not a forced doctrine

The following table records biblical actions, not exclusive job descriptions. The same divine work may be attributed to more than one Person, and the passages must govern each statement.

| Biblical reference | Explicit relation or action | What the text prevents us from saying |
|---|---|---|
| Father — John 6:37, 44, 65 | Gives, draws, grants | Human will independently originates coming |
| Father — John 5:21, 26 | Quickens; has life in Himself and gives to the Son to have life in Himself | The Father is absent from life-giving work |
| Son — John 1:1, 14 | The Word is God and becomes flesh | The Son is merely a created human messenger |
| Son — John 5:21, 25 | Quickens; His voice is heard and the dead live | The Son has no life-giving role |
| Son — John 10:17–18 | Lays down His life and takes it again, in relation to the command received from the Father | The Son's action is an autonomous creaturely act |
| Holy Ghost — John 6:63 | Quickeneth | Flesh or autonomous human will is the source of spiritual life |
| Holy Ghost — John 14:26; 15:26; 16:13–15 | Teaches, testifies, guides, glorifies the Son | The Spirit is an independent rival source detached from Father and Son |
| Spirit — Romans 8:11 | By the indwelling Spirit, God will quicken mortal bodies | The physical body is outside God's redemptive purpose |

This table is intentionally not titled “the three exclusive duties.” Scripture does not assign all divine action to one Person in isolation. It records distinct relations and actions within the one God.

## 10. The quickening question: who is the One recorded as quickening?

The previous investigation left Jimmy's phrase “ONE recorded NAME who Quickeneth” unresolved. This investigation clarifies the textual result:

- John 5:21 names the Father and the Son as quickening.
- John 6:63 names the Spirit as quickening.
- Romans 4:17 and 1 Timothy 6:13 name God as the One who quickens.
- Romans 8:11 describes God quickening mortal bodies by His Spirit.
- Ephesians 2:4–5 says God quickened those dead in sins.

These statements should not be forced into a contradiction, nor should their distinct grammatical subjects be erased.

If “ONE NAME” means one divine identity and source, the passages are consistent with the confession of one God. If Jimmy means a particular revealed name, the next study must examine the KJV's use of the name Jesus, the name of the Father, and the name of the Holy Ghost in their actual contexts. The investigation should not claim that the English verb “quickeneth” is attached to only one personal designation; the cited KJV passages plainly use it of more than one grammatical subject.

**Established finding:** No cited passage assigns the originating cause of quickening to autonomous human will.

## 11. GUA-W placement — no architectural changes

The established relationship remains:

**Author → Word → Creation → Witness**

This investigation adds no layer, operation, or state.

It examines these relationships within the existing map:
- **Author:** the one originating God.
- **Word:** the Word who was with God and was God, and who was made flesh.
- **Creation:** the physical creation and embodied human life, which remain within God's purpose.
- **Witness:** the creature who receives, hears, believes, responds, and is made alive.
- **Quickening:** life-giving action attributed in Scripture to God, Father, Son, and Spirit in their distinct textual relations.
- **Incarnation:** the Word made flesh.
- **Resurrection:** the bodily raising and transformation of Christ, with the promised redemption of believers' bodies.
- **Alignment:** 100% Alignment with the Author and His Word.
- **Drift:** any deviation from 100% Alignment.

“Spiritual → Physical → Spiritual” remains Jimmy's proposed descriptive shorthand. The investigation refines it because resurrection is bodily and the Spirit's work includes the redemption of the body. A more text-sensitive sequence is:

**Word with God → Word made flesh → bodily death → bodily resurrection/glorification → Spirit-given life and the promised redemption of mortal bodies.**

This is a sequence of biblical events and relations, not a new GUA-W runtime chain.

## 12. The Four-Question Study Method

### 1. What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?

AI must ask:
- What does each passage actually say about Father, Son, and Holy Ghost?
- Who is the grammatical subject of each action?
- Does the text describe identity, relation, mission, incarnation, indwelling, resurrection, or quickening?
- Is a proposed “duty” exclusive, or does Scripture attribute the action to more than one Person?
- Does “spiritual to physical” describe the incarnation, and does “back again” preserve bodily resurrection?
- Does the explanation maintain one God without erasing the distinctions Scripture makes?

### 2. What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

One originating God remains the Source. The Word is not a derivative human invention. The Word was made flesh by God's action. The Father, Son, and Holy Ghost are distinctly named and related in Scripture. Quickening belongs to divine action, not autonomous human will. The physical body is not discarded in the resurrection hope.

### 3. What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

John 1 describes the Word as God and the Word made flesh. John 5 attributes quickening to the Father and Son. John 6 attributes quickening to the Spirit and describes the Father's drawing and granting. John 14–16 records the relations among Father, Son, and Comforter. Luke 24 and John 20 affirm the risen Christ's bodily reality. Romans 8 and 1 Corinthians 15 connect divine life-giving work with bodily redemption and resurrection.

### 4. What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

A Witness can confuse unity with sameness, distinction with separation, incarnation with mere appearance, spiritual life with disembodiment, or human response with quickening. AI can make the same errors by compressing distinct passages into an oversimplified three-duty chart. The Witness must preserve what is stated, distinguish what is inferred, and leave unresolved what the text does not settle.

## 14. Findings and unresolved questions

### Findings

1. John 1:1 identifies the Word as God and as with God.
2. John 1:14 says the Word was made flesh and dwelt among us.
3. John 5:21 explicitly attributes quickening to both the Father and the Son.
4. John 6:63 explicitly says the Spirit quickens.
5. John 14–16 describes the Father, Son, and Comforter in distinct relations and missions.
6. The biblical passages do not support treating the three as three gods.
7. The passages also do not permit erasing the distinctions between Father, Son, and Spirit.
8. The incarnation is a real assumption of human flesh, not merely a symbolic appearance.
9. The risen Christ is bodily: Luke 24:39 speaks of flesh and bones, and John 20 records His wounds.
10. “Spiritual body” in 1 Corinthians 15 remains a body; it does not mean a non-body.
11. Romans 8:11 connects the Spirit's indwelling with God's promise to quicken mortal bodies.
12. Quickening is divine action; autonomous human will is not named as its originating cause.
13. The “three duties” proposal must remain a provisional summary because Scripture attributes overlapping actions to Father, Son, and Spirit.
14. The phrase “Spiritual → Physical → Spiritual” needs refinement so that bodily resurrection and redemption are not mistaken for abandonment of the physical.

### Unresolved questions

- How does the one divine identity relate to the distinct sending, being sent, proceeding, and testifying described in Scripture?
- How should John 5:21's Father-and-Son quickening and John 6:63's Spirit quickening be expressed without making the Persons interchangeable?
- How does the incarnation's Spiritual-to-Physical movement relate to the bodily resurrection without implying that the physical is discarded?
