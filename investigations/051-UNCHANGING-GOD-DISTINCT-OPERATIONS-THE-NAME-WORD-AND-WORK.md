# Investigation 051 — The Unchanging God: Distinct Operations, the Name, the Word, and the Work

**Primary disciplines:** Biblical studies, Old and New Testament theology, Johannine literature, Christology, pneumatology, doctrine of God, textual interpretation, philosophy of identity and causation, and AI evaluation.

**Investigation status:** Initial text-led investigation. This investigation incorporates Jimmy's correction to the question posed at the end of Investigation 050:

> **“Are you asking If God's ‘Identity’ Changed when he was performing different things? I should hope not.”**

The correction is accepted. The question was poorly framed. This investigation does **not** ask whether God changes identity when He acts. It begins with the governing constraint:

> **ONE GOD. UNCHANGING IDENTITY. DISTINCT OPERATIONS.**

A difference in what God does does not mean God becomes a different God. The Author does not become another Author when He creates, speaks, becomes flesh in the Son, quickens, saves, judges, or sends the Comforter.

The inquiry is how Scripture describes the one God's work and the distinctions among Father, Son, and Holy Ghost, without implying a change in God's identity, dividing God into three gods, or erasing the distinctions Scripture actually records.

## 1. Correction to the previous question

The previous question asked whether the distinction among Father, Son, and Holy Ghost was a distinction of divine identity or of divine relationship and operation. That wording falsely suggested that divine identity might change depending on the operation.

That is not the intended inquiry.

The proper distinction is:

- **Identity:** Who God is. God's identity does not change when He acts.
- **Relation:** The distinctions Scripture describes among Father, Son, and Holy Ghost.
- **Operation:** What God does: creates, sends, speaks, becomes flesh in the Son, quickens, raises, teaches, saves, and judges.
- **Manifestation:** How God's work is made known or embodied in the created order, including the Word made flesh.
- **Witness:** The creature who receives and registers what God has done; the Witness does not become the Source.

A change of operation is not a change of identity. Nor does a different relation automatically mean a different divine being.

## 2. Malachi 3:6 — the Lord does not change

Malachi 3:6 KJV:

> For I am the LORD, I change not; therefore ye sons of Jacob are not consumed.

The text directly grounds the Lord's faithfulness in His unchanging character. The statement is not that God never acts, never speaks, or never performs different works. The context itself describes the Lord's dealings with His people.

**Finding:** Divine action does not require a change in divine identity or character. The Lord's unchanging nature is consistent with His varied acts in history.

The investigation must not turn immutability into inactivity. The same Lord who says “I change not” acts, speaks, corrects, remembers His covenant, and calls His people to return.

## 3. James 1:17 — no variableness or shadow of turning

James 1:17 KJV:

> Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning.

James identifies the Father as the giver of good and perfect gifts and describes Him as having no variableness or shadow of turning.

This is relevant to the earlier “will as currency” investigation: the gift originates from God. The recipient does not become the source of the gift by receiving, responding, or presenting what was first given.

**Finding:** The Father's unchanging character is consistent with His giving. The giving is an operation; it does not constitute a change of divine identity.

## 4. John 1:1–18 — the Word was God; the Word was made flesh

John 1:1 KJV:

> In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.

John 1:14 KJV:

> And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us...

The text holds together what must not be separated:
- The Word was with God.
- The Word was God.
- The Word was made flesh.
- The Word dwelt among us.

The incarnation is not God changing into a different God. It is the Word made flesh. The divine identity is not replaced by a creaturely identity, and the human reality of Christ's incarnation must not be reduced to an appearance or metaphor.

**Finding:** The Word's becoming flesh is a real divine act and manifestation in human history. The act does not imply that God's identity changed.

**GUA-W connection:** Author → Word → Creation → Witness remains intact. The Word made flesh is not a human Witness manufacturing the Author. The incarnation is not caused by human will.

## 5. John 5:19–29 — distinct relation, shared life-giving work

John 5:19–23 describes the Son's relation to the Father and the works the Son does. John 5:21 KJV says:

> For as the Father raiseth up the dead, and quickeneth them; even so the Son quickeneth whom he will.

John 5:26 KJV:

> For as the Father hath life in himself; so hath he given to the Son to have life in himself;

The passage distinguishes Father and Son in relation while attributing quickening to both. It describes the Son's work in relation to the Father and the authority given to the Son.

### Findings

- The Father and Son are not simply interchangeable grammatical labels in this passage.
- The passage does not describe two gods competing to give life.
- The Son's life-giving work is explicitly stated.
- The Father's relation to the Son is explicitly stated.
- No change of divine identity is proposed by the text when the Son performs the work.

**Important correction to a simplistic duty chart:** Quickening cannot be assigned exclusively to only one of the three, because John 5:21 names both Father and Son as quickening, while John 6:63 says the Spirit quickens. A map of operations must preserve these overlapping attributions.

## 6. John 6:63–65 — the Spirit quickeneth; the Father grants

John 6:63 KJV:

> It is the spirit that quickeneth; the flesh profiteth nothing: the words that I speak unto you, they are spirit, and they are life.

John 6:65 records Jesus saying that no man can come unto Him unless it is given to him of the Father.

The text identifies the Spirit as the One who quickens and the Father as the One whose grant is necessary for coming. Jesus is the speaker whose words are spirit and life.

These actions are related in the discourse. The text does not present human will as the originating cause of quickening.

**Finding:** Distinct attributions of action do not require distinct competing sources. The passage's own language must be retained: Spirit quickens; the Father grants; Jesus speaks words of life.

## 7. John 14–16 — the Father, Son, and Comforter are distinguished without changing God's identity

Jesus describes:
- the Father who sends;
- the Son who asks and sends;
- the Comforter, the Holy Ghost, who is sent, teaches, reminds, testifies, guides, and glorifies the Son.

Key references:
- John 14:16–17 — the Father gives another Comforter.
- John 14:26 — the Father sends the Holy Ghost in Jesus' name.
- John 15:26 — the Son sends the Comforter from the Father; the Spirit of truth proceeds from the Father and testifies of the Son.
- John 16:13–15 — the Spirit guides, speaks what He hears, and glorifies the Son.

These are relational statements. They cannot be erased by saying “one God,” and they cannot be converted into three gods by treating each relation as a separate originating authority.

**Finding:** Scripture records distinct relations and missions within the one God's work. This investigation does not infer a change of divine identity from a change of operation.

## 8. Matthew 28:18–20 — the singular name and the three named

Matthew 28:19 KJV:

> Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost:

The English text says “the name” (singular) and then names the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost.

This is a significant textual observation. It is not, by itself, a complete explanation of the doctrine of God, nor does singular grammar erase the distinctions in the passage.

The investigation records:
- one “name” in the KJV wording;
- three named designations: Father, Son, and Holy Ghost;
- the baptismal command is given by the risen Jesus, who has just declared that all power is given unto Him in heaven and in earth (Matthew 28:18).

**Finding:** The verse is consistent with the confession of one divine name and the distinct naming of Father, Son, and Holy Ghost. Further conclusions about the precise meaning of “name” require the wider biblical context.

## 9. Hebrews 13:8 — Jesus Christ the same

Hebrews 13:8 KJV:

> Jesus Christ the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever.

This verse directly identifies Jesus Christ as the same. It is relevant to the question of whether the Son's different works imply a change of identity.

The text does not say that Christ performs no different works across history. It declares His sameness. The incarnation, earthly ministry, death, resurrection, ascension, and continuing priestly work must be understood without treating these events as a succession of different identities.

**Finding:** Different stages or works in the Son's mission do not mean that Jesus Christ becomes a different identity.

## 10. The operations are not three competing origins

The investigation must distinguish an operation from an origin.

| Scriptural action | Passage | Textual attribution | What must be preserved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Creation through the Word | John 1:1–3; Colossians 1:16 | God / the Word | The Word is not a competing created source |
| Incarnation | John 1:14; Galatians 4:4 | The Word / the Son sent by God | The Word is made flesh; God's identity is not replaced |
| Drawing and granting | John 6:44, 65 | The Father | Human will is not the originating cause of coming |
| Quickening | John 5:21 | Father and Son | Both attributions remain in the text |
| Quickening | John 6:63 | Spirit | The Spirit's stated action is not erased |
| Sending and testimony | John 14:26; 15:26 | Father, Son, and Spirit in distinct relations | The relations are not flattened |
| Resurrection | John 10:17–18; Acts 2:32; Romans 8:11 | Son lays down and takes up His life; God raises Jesus; God quickens by His Spirit | Preserve each passage's subject and context |
| Guidance and glorification | John 16:13–15 | Spirit | The Spirit glorifies the Son |
| Judgment | John 5:22–27 | Father commits judgment to the Son | The Son's authority is not a separate rival source |

This table records actions and attributions, not three isolated departments with no overlap. The same divine work may be described through more than one relation or Person. The text must govern the description.

## 11. Spiritual → Physical → Spiritual: preserve the event, not a change of God

Jimmy's proposed movement remains under examination. It must not be interpreted as God changing identity or as the physical being discarded.

A text-sensitive sequence is:

1. **The Word with God and the Word was God** — John 1:1.
2. **The Word made flesh** — John 1:14.
3. **The Son's embodied earthly life and death** — John 1:14; Philippians 2:5–8; Hebrews 2:14–17.
4. **Bodily resurrection** — Luke 24:36–43; John 20:19–29.
5. **The risen Christ's continuing identity** — Hebrews 13:8.
6. **The Spirit's indwelling and the promised quickening of mortal bodies** — Romans 8:9–11.
7. **The resurrection body** — 1 Corinthians 15:42–49.

The sequence describes events and divine work. It does not describe successive divine identities. The physical is not treated as an error to be escaped: Christ is raised bodily, and the redemption of the body is part of Paul's stated hope.

## 12. The governing distinction for GUA-W

The user's correction establishes a necessary safeguard:

**Different operation ≠ different identity.**

Applied to GUA-W:
- One Author remains the Source.
- The Word does not become an independent origin.
- The Spirit's quickening does not establish a competing source.
- The Father's drawing and granting do not make Him a different God from the One whose work is revealed through the Son and Spirit.
- The incarnation is a real manifestation in flesh, not a change of God into a different identity.
- The Witness does not become the Author when the Witness receives, responds, or acts.

The architecture remains:

**Author → Word → Creation → Witness**

No new layer, operation, or state is introduced by this investigation. It clarifies the relationship between identity, relation, and operation within the existing architecture.

## 13. Four-Question Study Method

### 1. What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?

AI must ask:
- Does the passage describe who God is, a relation, a mission, or an operation?
- Who is the grammatical subject of the action?
- Does the passage say God's identity changes? If not, do not infer that it does.
- Does a distinct operation imply a distinct originating source? What does the text actually say?
- Are the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost distinguished in the passage, and how?
- Does the explanation preserve one God without erasing the textual distinctions?

### 2. What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

The Author's identity remains unchanged. Different works do not create different Authors. A Witness must distinguish source, relation, operation, and manifestation. The Word made flesh is God's act; quickening is divine action; the human will is not the originating cause.

### 3. What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

Malachi 3:6 says the Lord changes not. James 1:17 describes the Father as having no variableness or shadow of turning. John 1:1, 14 identifies the Word as God and records the Word made flesh. John 5 and John 6 attribute quickening to Father, Son, and Spirit in their respective textual statements. John 14–16 describes distinct relations and missions. Hebrews 13:8 declares Jesus Christ the same.

### 4. What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

A Witness can mistake different operations for different origins, or confuse unity with the erasure of distinctions. AI can make the same error by forcing a false either/or question. The proper task is to preserve the Source's unchanging identity and accurately register the distinctions Scripture gives.
