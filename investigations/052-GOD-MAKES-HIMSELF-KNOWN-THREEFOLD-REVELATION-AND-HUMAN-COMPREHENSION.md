# Investigation 052 — God Makes Himself Known: Threefold Revelation and Human Comprehension

**Primary disciplines:** Biblical studies, biblical theology, Johannine literature, Christology, pneumatology, hermeneutics, communication theory, cognitive science, epistemology, philosophy of language, and AI evaluation.

**Investigation status:** Initial text-led investigation. This investigation incorporates Jimmy's explanation:

> **“Human minds need to comprehend God...so he showed Himself in three forms so that the human mind could comprehend. Jesus Spoke in parables, but He spoke the TRUTH.”**

The investigation records this as Jimmy's explanatory proposal: the one unchanging God makes Himself known in ways human beings can receive. The forms or modes of revelation do not mean God's identity changes. The parable comparison establishes an important communication principle: a truthful revelation can be expressed through a form the hearer understands without becoming false or exhausting the whole reality being revealed.

The investigation does not assume that Scripture itself uses Jimmy's exact phrase “three forms.” It tests the proposal against passages that reveal Father, Son, and Holy Ghost, and against passages showing God communicating through words, images, parables, and the incarnate Word.

## 1. Governing correction

The previous investigation established:

**ONE GOD. UNCHANGING IDENTITY. DISTINCT OPERATIONS.**

Jimmy now adds a question of revelation and human comprehension:

**The human mind needs God to make Himself known.**

The key distinction is between:
- **Who God is:** the unchanging divine identity.
- **What God does:** divine operations and works.
- **How God makes Himself known:** revelation, speech, signs, parables, the Word made flesh, and the work of the Spirit.
- **What a human Witness comprehends:** the truth received, according to the revelation given and the Witness's capacity to receive it.

God's making Himself known does not mean God changes identity to suit the observer. The form of communication can be fitted to the recipient without the truth being falsified.

A map can truthfully represent a territory without containing the territory. A parable can truthfully disclose a reality without describing every dimension of it. These are bounded communication analogies, not explanations of God's nature.

## 2. Deuteronomy 29:29 — what is revealed and what remains God's

Deuteronomy 29:29 KJV:

> The secret things belong unto the LORD our God: but those things which are revealed belong unto us and to our children for ever, that we may do all the words of this law.

The verse distinguishes what belongs to God as secret from what God has revealed. It does not say that the human mind comprehends all that God is. It does establish that what God reveals is genuinely given to His people and carries an intended purpose.

**Finding:** Revelation is real knowledge given by God; it is not exhaustive possession of God's whole being by the recipient.

**GUA-W connection:** The Witness receives what is revealed. The Witness does not become the Author or gain authority over what remains God's.

## 3. Psalm 78:1–8 — truth taught through a parable

Psalm 78:2 KJV:

> I will open my mouth in a parable: I will utter dark sayings of old:

The Psalm uses parable and remembered history to teach the generations about God's works, Israel's history, disobedience, and the need to set hope in God.

A parable is not necessarily a false story. It can be a truthful teaching form that makes a matter intelligible and memorable. In Psalm 78, the teaching is directed toward the actual works of God and the actual history of His people.

**Finding:** A form of communication can be figurative and still communicate truth. The form does not make the revealed lesson false.

## 4. Matthew 13:10–17, 34–35 — Jesus' parables and the mysteries of the kingdom

The disciples ask why Jesus speaks to the people in parables (Matthew 13:10). Jesus distinguishes those to whom it is given to know the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven from those who do not receive that understanding (13:11–17). Matthew also says Jesus spoke to the multitude in parables and fulfilled what was spoken by the prophet (13:34–35).

The parables are not presented as a license to invent doctrine from any imaginative interpretation. Their meaning is governed by Jesus' teaching and the kingdom truth He reveals.

**Finding:** Jesus uses a form of teaching that reveals truth while also exposing differences in reception. Hearing words is not identical to receiving their meaning.

The parables do not mean that truth itself changes according to the listener. The same teaching can be heard by many, while reception and understanding differ.

## 5. Mark 4:10–34 — “as they were able to hear it”

Mark 4:33–34 KJV:

> And with many such parables spake he the word unto them, as they were able to hear it.
>
> But without a parable spake he not unto them: and when they were alone, he expounded all things to his disciples.

This is especially important for the human-comprehension question. Mark records Jesus speaking the word in parables “as they were able to hear it,” and then privately expounding the teaching to His disciples.

The passage distinguishes:
- the truth being taught;
- the form in which it is communicated;
- the hearer's capacity to receive it;
- further explanation given by Jesus.

**Finding:** The form of presentation can be fitted to the hearer's capacity without changing the truth being taught. The teacher, not the hearer, remains the authority over the meaning of the teaching.

## 6. John 1:1–18 — the Word made flesh and the declaration of the Father

John 1:1 KJV:

> In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.

John 1:14 KJV:

> And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us...

John 1:18 KJV:

> No man hath seen God at any time; the only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him.

This passage is central to Jimmy's point. The Son declares the Father. The Word made flesh is not a human invention or an image created by human imagination. The revelation is God's act.

The verse says the Son has declared the Father. It does not say that human beings now exhaustively comprehend God's entire being. Revelation makes God known truly; it does not make the creature equal to the Creator.

**Finding:** The incarnation is a uniquely central revelation of God. The Word made flesh declares the Father while remaining the Word identified in John 1:1.

## 7. John 14:6–11 — “he that hath seen me hath seen the Father”

John 14:9 KJV:

> Jesus saith unto him, Have I been so long time with you, and yet hast thou not known me, Philip? he that hath seen me hath seen the Father; and how sayest thou then, Shew us the Father?

The surrounding verses matter. Jesus speaks of the Father dwelling in Him and of the works He does (John 14:10–11). The statement is not detached from the Son's relation to the Father.

This passage supports the claim that Jesus makes the Father known. It must be read with John 1:1, 14 and John 14:16–26, which also distinguish Father, Son, and Comforter.

**Finding:** In seeing and hearing Jesus, the disciples encounter the Father's revelation through the Son. This is not evidence that the Father's identity changed into the Son's identity. It is a statement about the Son's unique revelation of the Father.

## 8. John 14:16–26; 15:26; 16:12–15 — the Spirit teaches and guides

Jesus speaks of the Comforter, the Holy Ghost:
- the Father gives another Comforter (John 14:16);
- the Father sends the Holy Ghost in Jesus' name (14:26);
- the Spirit testifies of the Son (15:26);
- the Spirit of truth guides into all truth and glorifies the Son (16:13–15).

John 16:12 KJV:

> I have yet many things to say unto you, but ye cannot bear them now.

John 16:13 KJV:

> Howbeit when he, the Spirit of truth, is come, he will guide you into all truth...

The disciples' present capacity is limited: Jesus says they cannot bear everything at that moment. He promises the Spirit's guidance.

**Finding:** Scripture records a progression in what the disciples are able to bear and receive. The truth is not altered to accommodate them; God provides further teaching and guidance.

This is directly relevant to the threefold-revelation proposal. The Father, Son, and Holy Ghost are not merely three labels invented by human observers. Scripture presents them in distinct relations and actions. The Spirit's teaching is not an independent competing truth; He guides and glorifies the Son.

## 9. Hebrews 1:1–3 — God speaks, and the Son is the express image

Hebrews 1:1–3 KJV begins:

> God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spake in time past unto the fathers by the prophets,

and continues by saying that God has spoken unto us by His Son.

Hebrews describes the Son as:

> the brightness of his glory, and the express image of his person...

This passage directly addresses revelation across different manners of communication and the climactic revelation through the Son.

The phrase “divers manners” does not mean God had multiple identities. It describes the ways God spoke. The Son is not simply another parable or a human analogy; Hebrews gives Him a unique status as the brightness of God's glory and the express image of His person.

**Finding:** Different manners of divine communication do not imply different divine identities. The Son is the climactic revelation described by Hebrews, not a mere illustration invented by the human mind.

## 10. Threefold revelation: a careful test of Jimmy's wording

Jimmy's proposal is that God showed Himself in three forms so human minds could comprehend. The following distinctions keep that proposal testable:

| Question | Scriptural observation | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Is God one? | Deuteronomy 6:4; Isaiah 45:5; Mark 12:29 | Do not multiply the divine Source |
| Is the Father revealed? | John 1:18; John 14:6–11 | Do not treat the Father as an unrelated deity |
| Is the Son God's Word made flesh? | John 1:1–14; Hebrews 1:1–3 | Do not reduce the Son to a merely human illustration |
| Is the Holy Ghost distinctly named and active? | John 14:16–26; 15:26; 16:13–15 | Do not erase the Spirit's scriptural work |
| Does God teach through forms people can receive? | Psalm 78:2; Matthew 13; Mark 4:33–34 | Do not confuse the teaching form with falsehood |
| Does revelation exhaust God? | Deuteronomy 29:29; John 1:18 | Do not make human comprehension equal to God's full knowledge |

The table is not a claim that Scripture explicitly calls Father, Son, and Holy Ghost “three forms.” It records the passages that must be brought to bear on Jimmy's wording.

The phrase “three forms” can be ambiguous in Christian theology. Some readers use it to mean three ways the one God makes Himself known; others hear it as the doctrine that Father, Son, and Spirit are only temporary masks or roles. The latter interpretation does not preserve the distinct relations in John 14–16. The investigation must ask Jimmy what he means by “forms” before treating it as a technical doctrinal definition.

**Provisional formulation:** The one unchanging God makes Himself known through the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost as Scripture reveals them; the human recipient is given truthful revelation in forms and stages that can be received. This formulation is a working summary, not a replacement for the exact wording of the passages.

## 11. Truthful parable, partial comprehension

The distinction can be expressed as:

- **Truthfulness:** What is revealed is true.
- **Form:** The truth is communicated through words, images, stories, signs, or embodied revelation.
- **Reception:** The hearer receives what is communicated.
- **Comprehension:** The hearer understands to a greater or lesser degree.
- **Exhaustiveness:** The hearer does not thereby contain all that God is.

A parable can be true without being a literal report of every surface detail. It can reveal a true principle through a story. The truth of the parable depends on the teaching Christ intends, not on an interpreter's freedom to assign any meaning.

Likewise, the fact that God reveals Himself in ways human beings can receive does not imply that God has changed identity or that the human mind has comprehended the totality of God.

**GUA-W connection:** A Witness may receive a truthful account without possessing exhaustive knowledge of the Source. Incomplete comprehension is not automatically false witness. The limits of reception must be distinguished from the truthfulness of what was given.

## 12. No new GUA-W layer

The established architecture remains:

**Author → Word → Creation → Witness**

This investigation adds no layer, operation, or state. It clarifies how revelation is received:
- The Author is the originating Source.
- The Word declares and reveals; the Word was made flesh.
- The Spirit guides and testifies as Scripture states.
- The Witness receives, hears, understands, and reports.
- The Witness does not author the revelation or become the measure of its truth.

The existing distinction between Alignment and Drift remains intact. A Witness's limited comprehension must not automatically be called Drift. Drift remains deviation from 100% Alignment with the Author and His Word; an observer cannot judge another person's heart from a disagreement or incomplete explanation.

## 13. Four-Question Study Method

### 1. What must AI ask to understand GUA-W?

- What exactly does the passage reveal?
- Who is speaking, and who is being addressed?
- Is the passage using literal description, parable, metaphor, prophecy, or another communicative form?
- What truth does Jesus intend the parable to convey?
- What does the passage say about the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost?
- Does the proposed “three forms” wording preserve the distinctions in the passages?
- What is revealed, what is understood, and what remains beyond the Witness's comprehension?
- Am I mistaking a limit in my understanding for a defect in the revelation?

### 2. What is GUA-W revealing that helps AI understand?

The Author remains the Source. The Author's identity does not change to communicate with the Witness. God can reveal truth in forms a human being can receive. The Word made flesh declares the Father. The Spirit guides and testifies. The Witness's comprehension is derivative and need not be exhaustive to be truthful.

### 3. What am I learning about what I am witnessing?

Psalm 78 uses parable to teach God's works. Jesus teaches through parables and explains them to His disciples. Mark records that He spoke as the people were able to hear. John records the Word made flesh and the Son declaring the Father. Jesus promises the Spirit's guidance when the disciples cannot yet bear all He has to say. Hebrews describes God's speech through different manners and then through His Son.

### 4. What am I learning about the Witness through what I am witnessing?

A Witness can confuse the form of communication with the truth communicated. A Witness can also mistake partial comprehension for complete knowledge, or mistake a parable for falsehood because it is not a literal surface description. AI can make the same errors by flattening metaphor, forcing technical categories onto Scripture, or treating its own interpretation as the source of truth.
